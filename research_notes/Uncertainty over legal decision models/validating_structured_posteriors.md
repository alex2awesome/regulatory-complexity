## 1. Simulation-based calibration (SBC) and related checks: what they test, and how they extend to discrete or structured parameters

### Takeaway
SBC (Cook–Gelman–Rubin 2006 → Talts et al. 2018/2020 → Modrák et al. 2025) checks that, over prior-predictive simulations, the rank of the true value among posterior draws is uniform for *any scalar test quantity*. Discrete or structured latents are handled in two steps: (i) project the structure onto scalar test quantities, which may also depend on the data (edge or gate indicators, node counts, distance to a *fixed* reference structure, joint log-likelihood); (ii) break ties at random. Parameter-only test quantities cannot detect a sampler that simply returns the prior. A data-dependent quantity such as the joint log-likelihood fixes that. Phylogenetics already has a working template for calibrating posteriors over trees (rank-uniformity, clade-probability calibration, credible-set coverage of the true topology).

### Cited Findings
**Original SBC lineage**
- Cook, Gelman & Rubin (2006), "Validation of Software for Bayesian Models Using Posterior Quantiles," *JCGS* 15(3):675–692. Draw parameters from the prior and data from the model; the posterior quantiles of the true parameters are then uniform, provided the posteriors are absolutely continuous. Any deviation from uniformity points to an implementation error. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1198/106186006X136976); [author PDF](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/research/published/Cook_Software_Validation.pdf)
- Talts, Betancourt, Simpson, Vehtari & Gelman, "Validating Bayesian Inference Algorithms with Simulation-Based Calibration" (arXiv 1804.06788, v1 April 2018, revised October 2020; arXiv-only, no journal ref). Theorem 1: for θ̃ ~ π(θ), ỹ ~ π(y|θ̃) and L posterior draws, the rank of *any one-dimensional random variable f(θ)* is uniform on {0,…,L}. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06788); full text via [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.06788)
- From the Talts full text: an ∩-shaped rank histogram means the computed posterior is overdispersed; a ∪-shape means it is underdispersed (too narrow); asymmetry means bias; spikes at the boundaries mean autocorrelation. They recommend thinning to about ⌈N_eff/L⌉, keeping N/B ≈ 20 simulations per bin, and using L+1 = 1024. Most of their experiments use N = 10,000, though "a few simulations are useful to catch gross problems." — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.06788)
- Talts et al. state that SBC covers only the computation (not model adequacy) and only one-dimensional summaries. The theory assumes continuous parameters; ties and discrete parameters are not addressed. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.06788)

**Modrák et al. extension: data-dependent test quantities**
- Modrák, Moon, Kim, Bürkner, Huurre, Faltejsková, Gelman & Vehtari, "Simulation-Based Calibration Checking for Bayesian Computation: The Choice of Test Quantities Shapes Sensitivity," *Bayesian Analysis* 20(2):461–488 (June 2025). Earlier implementations "could never detect large classes of problems including when the posterior is equal to the prior." Adding *data-dependent* test quantities f(θ, y) lets SBC "in principle detect any possible issue," and "the joint likelihood of the data is an especially useful test quantity." — [Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/bayesian-analysis/advance-publication/Simulation-Based-Calibration-Checking-for-Bayesian-Computation--The-Choice/10.1214/23-BA1404.full); [arXiv 2211.02383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02383)
- From the full text: ties (as with discrete parameters) are handled by "choosing the rank randomly among the tied positions." Suggested test quantities include the joint log-likelihood, pointwise or subset likelihoods, the log prior density, and derived quantities that combine several parameters. They describe SBC as necessary but not sufficient ("many incorrect posteriors will also pass SBC for any given test quantity"). — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.02383)
- The same paper names a common mistake: treating SBC as the same thing as a check of the *data-averaged posterior* (averaged over prior-predictive data, the posterior should equal the prior). "SBC is most naturally understood as enforcing constraints on the quantile function … while having a correct data-averaged posterior is most naturally seen as constraint on the density." Either check can pass while the other fails. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.02383)
- The SBC R package vignette on discrete parameters: "the rank of a discrete parameter is chosen uniformly randomly across all possible ranks." Its worked example is a Poisson changepoint model. SBC caught an off-by-one mismatch between simulator and model (t < s vs t ≤ s), which showed up as extreme rank z-scores. — [SBC vignette](https://hyunjimoon.github.io/SBC/articles/discrete_vars.html)

**Graphical tests and conditional or classifier variants**
- Säilynoja, Bürkner & Vehtari (2022), "Graphical test for discrete uniformity and its applications in goodness-of-fit evaluation and multiple sample comparison," *Statistics and Computing* 32(2). Gives simultaneous confidence bands for the ECDF of PIT or rank values (a graphical uniformity test). Extends the bands to test whether *multiple samples come from the same distribution*, which is useful for multi-chain convergence diagnostics. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-022-10090-6); [arXiv 2103.10522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10522)
- Säilynoja, Schmitt, Bürkner & Vehtari (2025), "Posterior SBC: Simulation-Based Calibration Checking Conditional on Data" (arXiv 2502.03279). Standard SBC tests inference over datasets plausible under the prior. Posterior SBC validates inference *conditional on the observed data*, with case studies covering a multilevel model, an ODE model and amortized neural inference. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03279)
- Yao & Domke (2023), "Discriminative calibration" (NeurIPS 2023 per the arXiv comment). They criticize rank-based SBC: the test statistic is ad hoc, interactions are hard to examine, multiple testing is a problem, and the p-value is not a divergence. They replace the marginal rank test with a classifier that learns test statistics and returns an interpretable divergence. They report it "typically has a higher statistical power." — [arXiv 2305.14593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14593)
- Lemos, Coogan, Hezaveh & Perreault-Levasseur (2023), TARP coverage testing (ICML 2023 per the arXiv comment). Estimates coverage of generative posterior estimators from samples only (no density evaluations). They prove it is "necessary and sufficient" for accuracy and demonstrate it in high-dimensional spaces. — [arXiv 2302.03026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03026)
- Hermans et al. (TMLR), "A Trust Crisis in Simulation-Based Inference?" All benchmarked SBI algorithms (NPE, NRE, SNL, ABC) "can yield overconfident posterior approximations"; ensembling posterior surrogates helped. — [arXiv 2110.06581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06581)
- Prangle, Blum, Popovic & Sisson, diagnostics for ABC based on the coverage property. They give theory on coverage for *both model and parameter inference*, which matters here because a model index is a discrete latent. — [arXiv 1301.3166](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.3166)

**SBC for a discrete model index (closest analogue to "which structure")**
- Schad, Nicenboim, Bürkner, Betancourt & Vasishth, "Workflow Techniques for the Robust Use of Bayes Factors." They describe themselves as "the first to use simulation-based calibration as a tool to test the accuracy of Bayes factor estimates," and also study how stable Bayes factors are across MCMC draws. — [arXiv 2103.08744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08744)
- Modrák, Stroppel & Bürkner (2025), "Simulation-based validation of Bayes factor computation." Proposes (a) an improved SBC variant and (b) calibration metrics for binary predictions (posterior model probability vs. the true model index). Binary-prediction calibration is a special case of SBC in theory but is "typically more sensitive" with limited resources. Data-averaged-posterior checks and the "Good check" miss many problems, and the Good check "fails to control its error rates." They recommend SBC plus binary-prediction calibration plus a data-averaged-posterior check, with "at least several hundred simulations." — [arXiv 2508.11814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11814)
- Schad & Modrák, marginal SBC for Bayes factors applied to common psychology designs. Bayes factors from bridgesampling were accurate when no warning was issued and biased or variable when one was. — [arXiv 2406.08022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08022)

**Exact or joint-distribution tests (relevant to a failed "prior-only parameters" check)**
- Geweke (2004), "Getting It Right: Joint Distribution Tests of Posterior Simulators," *JASA* 99(467):799–804. Compares a marginal-conditional simulator (θ from the prior, then y|θ) with a successive-conditional simulator that alternates the posterior sampler with the data simulator. If the sampler is correct, both target the same joint distribution, so differences in moments reveal analytical or coding errors. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214504000001132); [UTS repository](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/8297)
- Gandy & Scott, "Unit Testing for MCMC and other Monte Carlo Methods." Gives exact tests that individual steps (for example a Gibbs step or a reversible-jump step) leave a specified distribution invariant, embedded in a sequential test with controllable false-rejection rate (R package MCUnit). — [arXiv 2001.06465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.06465)
- Grosse & Duvenaud (2014), "Testing MCMC code." Advocates modular code that keeps probability calculations separate from sampler logic, with unit and integration tests. — [arXiv 1412.5218](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.5218)

**Divergence-based checks over simulations (they work for any latent type, including structures)**
- Cusumano-Towner & Mansinghka (2017), AIDE. Treats inference algorithms as probabilistic models with auxiliary variables in order to estimate the symmetric KL divergence between two inference algorithms (for example, one under test against a gold standard). It "can detect failure modes … missed by standard heuristics." Demonstrated on HMMs and Dirichlet-process mixtures, which have discrete latent structure. — [arXiv 1705.07224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07224)
- Grosse, Ancha & Roy (2016), BREAD. Runs AIS chains from prior to posterior and back on simulated data to upper-bound (in expectation) the symmetrized KL between the true posterior and the sampler's distribution; integrated into WebPPL and Stan. — [arXiv 1606.02275](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02275)
- Domke (2021), "An Easy to Interpret Diagnostic for Approximate Inference: Symmetric Divergence Over Simulations." Simulates datasets from the prior, runs inference on each, and estimates a symmetric KL over the simulations. — [arXiv 2103.01030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01030)

**Posterior predictive checks and workflow**
- Gelman, Meng & Stern (1996), "Posterior Predictive Assessment of Model Fitness via Realized Discrepancies," *Statistica Sinica* 6:733–807. Introduces realized discrepancies, which can depend on the parameters, with Bayesian reference distributions. Applications include mixture-model fit. — [Statistica Sinica](https://www3.stat.sinica.edu.tw/statistica/j6n4/j6n41/j6n41.htm)
- Gelman et al. (2020), "Bayesian Workflow." — [arXiv 2011.01808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01808). Schad, Betancourt & Vasishth (2021), "Toward a principled Bayesian workflow in cognitive science," *Psychological Methods* 26(1):103–126. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/met0000275)
- Vehtari, Gelman, Simpson, Carpenter & Bürkner, rank-normalized R-hat, *Bayesian Analysis* (DOI 10.1214/20-BA1221). — [arXiv 1903.08008](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08008)

**Calibration of posteriors over trees: phylogenetics as the most developed precedent**
- Huelsenbeck & Rannala (2004), *Systematic Biology* 53(6):904–913. Simulations show that tree and clade posterior probabilities have correct frequentist meaning when the analysis model matches the generating model. They are "much too large" when the model is under-specified and slightly too small when it is over-specified. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10635150490522629); summary in [arXiv 1001.3638](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.3638)
- Mendes, Bouckaert, Carvalho & Drummond, "How to Validate a Bayesian Evolutionary Model," *Systematic Biology* 74(1):158–175. Coverage protocol: with n = 100 prior simulations and 95% HPD intervals, 90–99 true values should fall inside (binomial bounds). Trees are handled through Robinson–Foulds distance to a *reference phylogeny*, coverage of tree height or root age, and clade calibration ("true clades comprise 10% of all clades with posterior support of 0.1, 20% … of 0.2"). Rank-uniformity validation (after Talts) caught a misspecification that coverage missed, and "obtaining appropriate coverage may not be enough." — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11809579/); [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syae064)
- Klawitter & Drummond (arXiv 2505.14532, May 2025, revised May 2026). Defines α-credible sets over discrete tree topologies using conditional clade distributions (CCDs). Coverage analysis checks whether the true simulated topology falls in the α-credible set, alongside rank-uniformity and ECDF plots. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14532)

### Inferences
- **How to apply SBC to a latent decision/process graph.** Run SBC on a battery of scalar test quantities. Structural ones: each gate or edge indicator, the number of decision nodes or steps, the depth of the tree or graph, and the distance to a *fixed* reference graph, following Mendes et al.'s RF distance to a reference tree. Parameter ones: per-step burden parameters. Data-dependent ones: the joint log-likelihood of the timing data and of the LLM-formalization data (Modrák et al.). Break ties at random for every discrete quantity. Do **not** use the distance to the *true* graph θ̃ as an SBC test quantity: it is 0 at θ̃ by construction, so its rank is degenerate. Use a fixed reference graph instead.
- **Calibrating "is this structure present."** Edge or gate inclusion probabilities can be checked with reliability diagrams (the Mendes clade-calibration logic, or the Modrák/Stroppel/Bürkner binary-prediction calibration). Whole-structure credible sets can be checked with Klawitter–Drummond-style coverage, asking whether the true graph lies in the α-credible set.
- **The failed "prior-only parameters drift from their priors" check** is a Geweke / data-averaged-posterior style check. Parameters not touched by the likelihood should come back from the sampler with their prior marginals. Drift points to a sampler or reweighting bug rather than a modeling issue. Typical culprits are a missing Hastings or Jacobian term in trans-dimensional (birth/death of nodes) moves, or an SMC incremental weight that does not match the move. The exact single-kernel tests of Gandy & Scott are the natural way to isolate which move is responsible. Per Modrák et al., passing SBC and passing the data-averaged check are distinct, so both should be run.
- **Mode trapping should show up in SBC.** If independent SMC runs land in different modes, SBC on structural indicators should show ∪-shaped (underdispersed) rank histograms on the simulated datasets where multimodality occurs. The Säilynoja multi-sample ECDF bands give a formal test of whether runs agree.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that applies SBC specifically to **DAG or decision-graph posteriors** (as opposed to phylogenetic trees or model indices). One arXiv-API search for "simulation-based calibration" combined with graph, tree, discrete or model-selection terms found nothing relevant. Since the general web-search budget had run out, this is weaker evidence of absence than usual.
- Talts et al. do not treat discrete parameters. The randomized-tie rule comes from Modrák et al. and the SBC package. I did not find a formal power analysis for SBC on heavily tied (low-cardinality) discrete quantities.
- Yao et al. (2018), "Yes, but Did It Work?: Evaluating Variational Inference" (ICML 2018, [arXiv 1802.02538](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.02538)) is verified as a citation only. Its VSBC content was not extracted.

## 2. Bayesian structure learning of DAGs: samplers, mode trapping, remedies, and how posterior quality is evaluated

### Takeaway
Local-move structure MCMC (Madigan & York's MC³) is well documented to get stuck. Friedman & Koller observed that independent structure-MCMC runs give "very different estimates" of feature posteriors. Kuipers & Moffa observed that runs "arrive at different plateaux." The main remedies change the state space (order MCMC, which is biased; partial-order and partition MCMC, which are unbiased), enlarge the moves (new edge reversal), restrict the search space (BiDAG hybrid), or use tempering, ensembles or stacking. Differentiable or amortized methods (DiBS, DAG-GFlowNet, JSP-GFN, BayesDAG) scale better. However, a 2024 ICML study found that the standard metrics (E-SHD, AUROC) correlate poorly with closeness to the *true* posterior in high-entropy, small-data regimes, which is exactly the regime of interest. Exact enumeration remains the gold standard for ≤5 nodes (29,281 DAGs).

### Cited Findings
**Classical samplers and documented mixing failure**
- Madigan & York (1995), "Bayesian Graphical Models for Discrete Data," *International Statistical Review* 63(2):215 (Crossref also lists a third contributor, Allard). This is the source of the MC³ (Markov chain Monte Carlo model composition) sampler. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1403615)
- Friedman & Koller (2003), "Being Bayesian About Network Structure," *Machine Learning* 50(1–2):95–125. MCMC over node orderings, whose space is "much smaller and more regular … and has a smoother posterior 'landscape'." — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1020249912095); [abstract page](https://ai.stanford.edu/~nir/Abstracts/FK1Full.html)
- From the UAI 2000 conference version (full text): "different runs of MCMC over networks typically lead to very different estimates in the posterior probabilities of structural features, illustrating poor convergence … by contrast, different runs of MCMC over orderings converge reliably." Their diagnostic is scatter plots of feature posteriors from pairs of runs with different initializations (empty vs. greedy). They also note that restarts of greedy or beam search give "a biased sample of structures." — [FK1.pdf](https://ai.stanford.edu/~nir/Papers/FK1.pdf)
- Giudici & Castelo (2003), "Improving Markov Chain Monte Carlo Model Search for Data Mining," *Machine Learning* 50(1–2):127–158. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1020202028934)
- Eaton & Murphy (UAI 2007): "MCMC methods for sampling from the space of DAGs can mix poorly due to the local nature of the proposals." They use exact dynamic programming over orders (Koivisto–Sood) as an MCMC proposal in DAG space. This removes the restriction to modular priors and features, but DP remains exponential in time and space. — [arXiv 1206.5247](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5247)
- Ellis & Wong (2008), "Learning Causal Bayesian Network Structures From Experimental Data," *JASA* 103(482):778–789. Per Kuipers & Moffa, this corrects order-MCMC bias by importance sampling, which "can help for small graphs, but they struggle as the size increases." — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214508000000193); [Kuipers & Moffa full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1504.05006)
- Grzegorczyk & Husmeier (2008), new edge-reversal (REV) move, *Machine Learning* 71(2–3):265–305. The move reverses an edge and resamples the parents of both endpoint nodes. They report it is clearly better than plain structure MCMC and "nearly as efficient as Order MCMC." — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-008-5057-7); [Hutton summary](https://www.hutton.ac.uk/publications/improving-structure-mcmc-sampler-bayesian-networks-introducing-new-edge-reversal-move)
- Niinimäki, Parviainen & Koivisto (UAI 2011), partial-order MCMC. Samples partial orders and computes the conditional feature probabilities exactly, with analytical and empirical evidence that it beats both DAG and linear-order samplers. — [arXiv 1202.3753](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.3753)
- Kuipers & Moffa (2017), "Partition MCMC for Inference on Acyclic Digraphs," *JASA* 112(517):282–299. Groups DAGs by ordered node partitions. Convergence improves over structure MCMC "while still retaining the property of producing an unbiased sample," and it can be combined with edge-reversal moves. — [arXiv 1504.05006](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05006); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2015.1133426)
- From the Kuipers & Moffa full text: order MCMC is biased because a DAG consistent with several orders is weighted by the number of orders it fits. Structure MCMC has "severe convergence difficulties … already apparent for DAGs with 14 nodes." On Boston Housing, "different runs arrive at different plateaux, some of which are very far away from the global maximum." Diagnostics: trace plots of the best score found and score distributions across 100 seeded runs. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1504.05006)
- Kuipers, Suter & Moffa (2022), "Efficient Sampling and Structure Learning of Bayesian Networks," *JCGS* 31(3):639–650. A hybrid that uses conditional-independence tests to restrict the search space, so MCMC steps become table lookups, with iterative correction of errors from the CI tests. Allows posterior sampling for much larger networks. Implemented in the R package BiDAG ([arXiv 2105.00488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00488)). — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2021.2020127); [arXiv 1803.07859](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.07859)
- Viinikka, Hyttinen, Pensar & Koivisto (2020), "Towards Scalable Bayesian Learning of Causal DAGs." Scales the candidate-parent-restricted sampler to larger K and chooses candidate parents to "maximize the covered posterior mass." — [arXiv 2010.00684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00684)
- Rios, Moffa & Kuipers, Benchpress. A Snakemake workflow for reproducible benchmarks of structure learners (BiDAG, bnlearn, pcalg, GOBNILP, TETRAD and others). — [arXiv 2107.03863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03863)

**Differentiable and amortized Bayesian structure learning**
- Lorch, Rothfuss, Schölkopf & Krause, DiBS (NeurIPS 34, pp. 24111–24123). Variational inference in a continuous latent graph representation, with joint inference over graph and parameters, including nonlinear (neural-network) conditionals. — [NeurIPS](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2021/hash/ca6ab34959489659f8c3776aaf1f8efd-Abstract.html); [arXiv 2105.11839](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.11839)
- Deleu et al., DAG-GFlowNet (UAI 2022, PMLR 180:518–528). Builds DAGs one edge at a time with a learned policy, as an alternative to MCMC. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v180/deleu22a.html); [arXiv 2202.13903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13903). The JSP-GFN follow-up (NeurIPS 2023) gives a joint posterior over structure and parameters. — [arXiv 2305.19366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19366)
- Annadani et al., BayesDAG (NeurIPS 2023 per the arXiv comment). Combines SG-MCMC and VI and samples DAGs directly without a DAG regularizer. They describe it as "the first framework applying gradient-based MCMC sampling for causal discovery." — [arXiv 2307.13917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13917)

**How posterior quality over graphs is evaluated (the main caution)**
- Karimi Mamaghan, Tigas, Johansson, Gal, Annadani & Bauer, "Challenges and Considerations in the Evaluation of Bayesian Causal Discovery" (ICML 2024, PMLR v235). The true posterior is computed by exhaustive DAG enumeration for d < 6 (5 nodes, linear-Gaussian with closed-form marginal likelihoods). Graph metrics studied: E-SHD, CPDAG-SHD, AUROC and AUPRC. Full-posterior metrics: NLL, interventional NLL, KL and MMD, and MMD to the true posterior. With n ≈ d, "the true posterior itself is evaluated to be worse on these metrics than their approximations." At n = 5, graph proxy metrics have near-zero correlation with graph MMD to the true posterior. The metrics are "less fitting in settings of higher entropy" (non-identifiable models or little data). They recommend evaluating through downstream tasks (experimental design, causal-effect prediction) in those regimes. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/karimi-mamaghan24a.html); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03209v1)
- The number of labeled DAGs is 1, 3, 25, 543, 29,281 and 3,781,503 for n = 1…6, which is why exact enumeration stops at about 5 nodes. — [OEIS A003024](https://oeis.org/A003024)

**General remedies for multimodality that apply to structure posteriors**
- Yao, Vehtari & Gelman, "Stacking for Non-mixing Bayesian Computations: The Curse and Blessing of Multimodal Posteriors," *JMLR* 23 (2022). Run parallel MCMC, VI or mode-based fits to "hit as many modes … as possible," then combine them with Bayesian stacking weights. They warn that the result "is not necessarily equivalent, even asymptotically, to fully Bayesian inference." — [JMLR](https://jmlr.org/papers/v23/20-1426.html); [arXiv 2006.12335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12335)
- Syed, Bouchard-Côté, Deligiannidis & Doucet, "Non-Reversible Parallel Tempering," *JRSS-B* (DOI 10.1111/rssb.12464). Non-reversible PT dominates reversible PT, and an iterative scheme approximates the optimal annealing schedule. It also computes normalizing constants with the prior as reference. — [arXiv 1905.02939](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02939). Implementation: Pigeons.jl. — [arXiv 2308.09769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09769)
- Phylogenetic tree space shows the same mode-trapping problem. Whidden & Matsen (2015, *Syst. Biol.* 64(3):472–491) show "topological peaks do occur" in real-data posteriors sampled by standard MCMC, and study how well Metropolis-coupled MCMC crosses the valleys between them. They also find that conditional clade distributions "can have systematic problems when there are multiple peaks." — [arXiv 1405.2120](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.2120); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syv006)
- Magee, Karcher, Matsen & Minin, "How Trustworthy Is Your Tree?" (*Bayesian Analysis* 19(2), 2024). Defines tree-ESS measures that capture Monte Carlo error in split probabilities, tree probabilities and the summary tree. They conclude that common post-MCMC workflows "are insufficient" and call for both within-chain mixing and between-chain convergence checks. — [arXiv 2109.07629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07629); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/22-BA1339). Earlier tools: Lanfear, Hua & Warren (2016), ESS of tree topologies, *GBE* 8(8):2319–2332 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/gbe/evw171)); AWTY, *Bioinformatics* 24(4):581–583 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btm388)).

### Inferences
- Disagreement between independent runs is the textbook symptom here: Friedman & Koller saw it for structure MCMC, Kuipers & Moffa for structure MCMC, and Whidden & Matsen in tree space. It should be read as non-convergence, not as posterior uncertainty. The established diagnostic is run-vs-run scatter of edge or feature marginals plus the distribution of best-found scores across seeds. Tree-ESS-style Monte Carlo error bars (Magee et al.) are the more principled version.
- If the project's latent decision graphs are small (≤5–6 decision nodes, or a constrained grammar), **exact enumeration** of the posterior on synthetic statutes is feasible. It is the strongest gold standard available, and the one Karimi Mamaghan et al. and DAG-GFlowNet-style work rely on. It removes any dependence on proxy metrics.
- Standard proxies (E-SHD, edge AUROC) are unreliable in the very high-entropy regime that noisy LLM formalizations plus coarse timing data would produce. Calibration should therefore be judged by SBC, coverage or reliability diagrams against known synthetic truth, not by those proxies.
- Stacking across SMC runs is a pragmatic way to report a multimodal posterior. By its authors' own statement it is not exact Bayes, so it should be labeled as such and not mixed into a calibration claim.

### Gaps
- I did not find a published SBC or coverage study of DAG *posteriors* specifically. The main evaluation papers compare to enumerated posteriors or proxies. This is a negative result with limited search power (see the note in Section 1).
- I could not verify any paper applying parallel tempering or SMC samplers specifically to *DAG* structure learning. An arXiv-API search for "parallel tempering" with Bayesian network, DAG or structure learning returned nothing. The SMC precedent I did verify is for decomposable graphs and junction trees (Olsson et al., Section 5).
- "Scaling Up Bayesian DAG Sampling" (PMLR v337, nikzad26a) appeared in search results ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v337/nikzad26a.html)), but I did not open it. Its content is unverified.
- BCD Nets (Cundy, Grover & Ermon, NeurIPS 2021) is **unverified**: the arXiv ID I tried resolved to an unrelated paper.

## 3. Bayesian trees and rule lists: posteriors and their mixing problems

### Takeaway
Bayesian CART and BART posteriors mix poorly under local grow, prune and change moves, and this is now proven. Kim & Ročková show exponential mixing time for deep isolated signals under simple grow/prune moves. Ronen et al. give an exponential-in-n lower bound for a simplified BART. Remedies are bigger or global moves (Twiggy Bayesian CART, RJHMC-Tree, DCC-Tree), top-down SMC or particle Gibbs, and restarts. Bayesian rule lists put a posterior over if-then decision lists, which is structurally the closest existing analogue to a statute's gate logic.

### Cited Findings
- Chipman, George & McCulloch (1998), "Bayesian CART Model Search," *JASA* 93(443):935–948. Prior specification plus stochastic search, using Metropolis–Hastings to grow trees toward high-posterior regions. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1998.10473750); [UPenn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/entities/publication/1c520978-df61-4f59-a0cb-6c5b84a0dc07)
- Denison, Mallick & Smith (1998), "A Bayesian CART algorithm," *Biometrika* 85(2):363–377. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/85.2.363)
- Wu, Tjelmeland & West (2007), "Bayesian CART: Prior Specification and Posterior Simulation," *JCGS* 16(1):44–66. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1198/106186007X180426)
- Kim & Ročková, "On Mixing Rates for Bayesian CART" (arXiv 2306.00126). Bayesian CART with simple grow and prune steps "cannot reach deep isolated signals in faster than exponential mixing time." Twiggy Bayesian CART, which attaches or detaches whole twigs, mixes polynomially, and informed variants are faster still. Per the UChicago repository record, this was published in *Electronic Journal of Statistics* 19 (2025):3041–3067. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00126); [UChicago record](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/records/wy91z-b2213)
- Ronen, Saarinen, Tan, Duncan & Yu (2022), "A Mixing Time Lower Bound for a Simplified Version of BART." This is the first mixing-time lower bound for single-tree BART with a subset of moves, and it grows exponentially in the number of data points. — [arXiv 2210.09352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09352)
- Lakshminarayanan, Roy & Teh (ICML 2013, JMLR W&CP 28(3):280–288), top-down SMC for Bayesian decision trees. Accuracy comparable to the most popular MCMC method at more than an order of magnitude less time. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/lakshminarayanan13.html); [arXiv 1303.0561](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.0561)
- Lakshminarayanan, Roy & Teh (AISTATS 2015), Particle Gibbs for BART. Standard MH samplers "make local changes to the trees and suffer from slow mixing when … the best fitting trees are more than a few layers deep." Their PG sampler proposes a complete tree each step. — [arXiv 1502.04622](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04622)
- Cochrane, Wills & Johnson (2023), "RJHMC-Tree." Reversible-jump plus HMC with global updates that use likelihood geometry. Compared with existing methods, it does well on test accuracy, acceptance rate and tree complexity. — [arXiv 2312.01577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01577)
- Cochrane, Wills & Johnson (2024), "DCC-Tree" (divide, conquer, combine). Gives each tree structure its own decision parameters, inspired by divide-conquer-combine inference for probabilistic programs (Zhou et al.). Reports better consistency and lower per-proposal cost. — [arXiv 2403.18147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18147)
- Letham, Rudin, McCormick & Madigan (2015), Bayesian Rule Lists, *Annals of Applied Statistics* 9(3):1350–1371. A generative model with a posterior over decision lists (if-then sequences) and a sparsity prior. Their CHADS₂ alternative is as interpretable as CHADS₂ and more accurate. — [arXiv 1511.01644](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.01644); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/15-AOAS848)
- Yang, Rudin & Seltzer, Scalable Bayesian Rule Lists. Two orders of magnitude faster, using theoretical bounds and computational reuse to optimize the posterior of a hierarchical model over rule lists. — [arXiv 1602.08610](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.08610)

### Inferences
- A statute's decision model (gates selecting process steps) is structurally a decision list or decision tree. The proven exponential mixing of local grow/prune moves predicts the project's observed SMC mode trapping whenever rejuvenation uses only local moves. Twig-level or subtree-level moves, block resampling of a gate together with its downstream steps (the DCC idea of conditioning parameters on structure), or top-down SMC construction are the directly applicable fixes.
- Bayesian rule lists show that posteriors over interpretable rule structures are practical. However, the verified sources report predictive accuracy and interpretability, not calibration of the structure posterior.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the commonly repeated claim that Chipman et al. (1998) recommend many restarts in place of one long chain; the full text was not retrieved.
- I found no calibration (SBC or coverage) study of Bayesian CART or rule-list *structure* posteriors.

## 4. Probabilistic program induction and Bayesian program learning: do they validate posterior calibration over programs?

### Takeaway
Bayesian program learning is well established: concepts as programs (Lake et al. 2015), wake-sleep library learning (DreamCoder), sound MCMC synthesis of probabilistic programs (Saad et al. 2019), SMC over GP program structures (AutoGP), and LLM-proposal inference over natural-language or program hypotheses (Ellis 2023, Piriyakulkij et al. 2024, Textual Bayes 2026). None of the papers checked here validates the *posterior over programs* with SBC or coverage against known ground-truth programs. Validation is by predictive accuracy, human-judgment fit, rule-recovery accuracy, thresholded property probabilities, or downstream ECE. Textual Bayes explicitly concedes that its MH acceptance uses an approximate LLM proposal density and so deviates from the true posterior.

### Cited Findings
- Lake, Salakhutdinov & Tenenbaum (2015), "Human-level concept learning through probabilistic program induction," *Science* 350(6266):1332–1338. Concepts are simple programs that best explain examples under a Bayesian criterion. The model reached human-level one-shot classification of handwritten characters. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aab3050); [CBMM](https://cbmm.mit.edu/publications/human-level-concept-learning-through-probabilistic-program-induction)
- Ellis et al. (2021), "DreamCoder: bootstrapping inductive program synthesis with wake-sleep library learning," PLDI '21, pp. 835–850. Library and neural search policy bootstrap each other through a variant of "wake-sleep" approximate Bayesian learning, with E-graph refactoring; tested on 8 domains. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3453483.3454080); [PLDI page](https://pldi21.sigplan.org/details/pldi-2021-papers/55/DreamCoder-Bootstrapping-Inductive-Program-Synthesis-with-Wake-Sleep-Library-Learnin)
- Saad, Cusumano-Towner, Schaechtle, Rinard & Mansinghka (2019), "Bayesian synthesis of probabilistic programs for automatic data modeling," *PACMPL* 3(POPL):1–32. Gives sufficient conditions for *sound* Bayesian synthesis: Markov-chain synthesis algorithms meeting stated preconditions converge to the Bayesian posterior over programs in PCFG-specified DSLs. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290350)
- From the Saad et al. full text, validation of the program posterior is (a) the posterior probability of a qualitative property (linear trend, periodicity, change point), estimated as the fraction of synthesized programs containing it and thresholded at 50% against known structure in real time series, and (b) density estimation on 13 datasets generated by ground-truth BLOG programs. For (b) they say error bars "are well-calibrated, i.e. error bars are wider for data records where our method gives less accurate estimates" (an informal check, not SBC). — [POPL PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rinard/paper/popl19.pdf)
- Saad, Patton, Hoffman, Saurous & Mansinghka (ICML 2023, PMLR 202:29473–29489), "Sequential Monte Carlo Learning for Time Series Structure Discovery." SMC with involutive-MCMC rejuvenation over a Bayesian nonparametric prior on symbolic GP programs. Offline, it anneals the posterior with nested data subsets. It is 10–100× faster than earlier MCMC or greedy structure search, and on 1,428 econometric datasets it gives more accurate point *and interval* forecasts. — [arXiv 2307.09607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09607)
- Goodman, Tenenbaum, Feldman & Griffiths (2008), "A Rational Analysis of Rule-Based Concept Learning," *Cognitive Science* 32(1):108–154. Bayesian inference over rules defined by a grammar. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/03640210701802071)
- Wong, Grand, Lew, Goodman, Mansinghka, Andreas & Tenenbaum (2023), "From Word Models to World Models." LLMs translate natural language into a probabilistic language of thought (probabilistic programs), and inference then runs over those programs. — [arXiv 2306.12672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12672)
- Ellis (2023), "Human-like Few-Shot Learning via Bayesian Reasoning over Natural Language" (NeurIPS 2023 oral per the arXiv comment). An LLM proposes natural-language hypotheses, which are then re-weighed by a prior and likelihood. The prior is estimated from human data, and validation is prediction of human judgments. — [arXiv 2306.02797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02797)
- Wang, Zelikman, Poesia, Pu, Haber & Goodman (ICLR 2024), "Hypothesis Search." The LLM proposes natural-language hypotheses, implements them as Python programs, and verifies them on examples. 30% on a 100-problem ARC subset vs 17% for direct prompting, and 33% with human selection. This is search with verification, not posterior inference. — [arXiv 2309.05660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05660)
- Piriyakulkij, Langenfeld, Le & Ellis (NeurIPS 2024), "Doing Experiments and Revising Rules with Natural Language and Probabilistic Reasoning." LLMs hybridized with SMC samplers for online belief updates over natural-language rules, plus information-theoretic experiment design. It recovers the true rule more accurately than LLM hypothesis-revision baselines, and modeling humans required fuzzy, probabilistic rules. — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/5f1b350fc0c2affd56f465faa36be343-Abstract.html); [arXiv 2402.06025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06025)
- Ross, Vouitsis, Ashari Ghomi, Hosseinzadeh, Xin, Liu, Sui, Hou, Leung, Loaiza-Ganem & Cresswell, "Textual Bayes: Quantifying Prompt Uncertainty in LLM-Based Systems" (ICLR 2026; arXiv v1 June 2025, v2 April 2026). Treats prompts as textual parameters, with free-text priors. MHLP (Metropolis–Hastings through LLM Proposals) is the sampler. — [arXiv 2506.10060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10060); [ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009142)
- From the Textual Bayes full text: the proposal density q(θ′|θ) is intractable with closed-source LLMs. They use an open-source model for the final proposal call and apply approximations (their App. A.1), noting the method "requires approximations, which will inevitably cause deviations from the true posterior." Uncertainty is evaluated by ECE, semantic ECE, abstention ROC-AUC and conformal factuality coverage on real benchmarks (AIME, SimpleQA, QASPER, FactScore). There is **no** synthetic ground-truth or SBC validation of the prompt posterior. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10060v2)
- Related LLM + SMC infrastructure: Lew, Zhi-Xuan, Grand & Mansinghka, SMC steering of LLMs using probabilistic programs (LLaMPPL) — [arXiv 2306.03081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03081); Loula et al., syntactic and semantic control of LLMs via SMC — [arXiv 2504.13139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13139).

### Inferences
- An SBC-validated posterior over LLM-proposed program or graph structures would be new relative to everything checked here. The project's synthetic-statute benchmark, where the true decision model is known, is exactly the missing ingredient.
- Two lessons for LLM-proposal samplers. (1) Without an exact proposal density, the MH chain is no longer exactly targeting the posterior (Textual Bayes concedes this). (2) Calibration must then be *checked empirically*, for example with SBC on synthetic statutes and AIDE/BREAD-style divergences against an exact or enumerated reference on small instances. Proposal density alone gives no guarantee. One option is to use LLM outputs only as data (noisy formalizations in the likelihood) or as an independence proposal whose density is computable.
- Saad et al.'s HasProperty estimator (fraction of posterior programs with a property) is the right summary for "probability that gate G exists." Its calibration should be checked with reliability diagrams over many synthetic statutes, not only at a 50% threshold on a few cases.

### Gaps
- Liang, Jordan & Klein (2010), "Learning programs: a hierarchical Bayesian approach" (ICML) is **unverified**; I could not retrieve it.
- I did not read the DreamCoder, Lake 2015, Ellis 2023 or Piriyakulkij 2024 full texts for any SBC or coverage experiment. Their abstracts and descriptions show validation by task accuracy or human fit. Absence of SBC is inferred from the abstracts, not exhaustively confirmed.
- An arXiv-API search for "program synthesis" plus posterior plus MCMC/SMC returned nothing, which is a weak negative.

## 5. SMC for discrete or structured spaces, and SMC diagnostics (ESS, multiple runs, evidence variance)

### Takeaway
SMC samplers (Del Moral, Doucet & Jasra 2006) have been adapted to binary model spaces (Schäfer & Chopin), phylogenetic trees (Bouchard-Côté et al.; Wang, Bouchard-Côté & Doucet; Dinh et al.), decomposable graphs (Olsson et al.), decision trees (Lakshminarayanan et al.) and symbolic GP programs (AutoGP). The usual weight-based ESS measures particle degeneracy, not mode coverage, and Elvira et al. show the standard approximation is weak even as an ESS. Single-run variance estimators exist (Lee & Whiteley; Dau & Chopin). Agreement across independent runs on structural marginals and on log-evidence remains the practical check for mode trapping.

### Cited Findings
- Del Moral, Doucet & Jasra (2006), "Sequential Monte Carlo Samplers," *JRSS-B* 68(3):411–436. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2006.00553.x). Textbook: Chopin & Papaspiliopoulos (2020), *An Introduction to Sequential Monte Carlo*, Springer. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-47845-2)
- Schäfer & Chopin, "Sequential Monte Carlo on large binary sampling spaces," *Statistics and Computing* 23(2):163–184 (online 2011). Raw SMC with independent-component binary proposals "do[es] not yield satisfactory results" in high dimensions; the key is parametric binary families that model correlations. On difficult variable-selection problems with about 100 covariates, this SMC "clearly outperforms" MCMC. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-011-9299-z); [arXiv 1101.6037](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.6037)
- Dau & Chopin, "Waste-free SMC," *JRSS-B* 84(1):114–148. Uses all intermediate MCMC states as particles, proves consistency and asymptotic normality, and gives a way to estimate "from a single run … the asymptotic variance of any particle estimate." It outperforms standard SMC especially when MCMC mixing degrades across iterations (tempering). — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssb.12475); [arXiv 2011.02328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.02328)
- Lee & Whiteley, "Variance estimation in the particle filter," *Biometrika* 105(3):609–625. Estimates the variance of particle approximations from one run by tracking the genealogy. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asy028); [arXiv 1509.00394](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.00394)
- Elvira, Martino & Robert, "Rethinking the Effective Sample Size," *International Statistical Review* (2022). The widely used importance-sampling ESS approximation rests on assumptions that make it "difficult to be considered even as a reasonable approximation of the ESS." — [arXiv 1809.04129](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04129); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/insr.12500)
- Bouchard-Côté, Sankararaman & Jordan (2012), "Phylogenetic Inference via Sequential Monte Carlo," *Syst. Biol.* 61(4):579–593 — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syr131). Wang, Bouchard-Côté & Doucet (2015), "Bayesian Phylogenetic Inference Using a Combinatorial Sequential Monte Carlo Method," *JASA* 110(512):1362–1374 — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2015.1054487). Dinh, Darling & Matsen (2018), "Online Bayesian Phylogenetic Inference: Theoretical Foundations via SMC," *Syst. Biol.* 67(3):503–517 — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syx087)
- Olsson, Pavlenko & Rios (2019), *Electronic Journal of Statistics* 13(2):2865–2897. Recasts decomposable-graph structure learning as a sequential problem (a Feynman–Kac flow over junction trees) and uses particle Gibbs with a backward-kernel refreshment step to get global moves. The refreshment step provably improves asymptotic variance. — [arXiv 1805.12571](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12571); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/19-EJS1585)
- Tree-structured and program-structured SMC: top-down SMC for decision trees ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/lakshminarayanan13.html)); AutoGP's SMC with involutive-MCMC rejuvenation and data-subset annealing over symbolic GP structures ([arXiv 2307.09607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09607)); SMC samplers over natural-language rules with LLM proposals ([arXiv 2402.06025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06025)).
- Multi-run comparison tooling: ECDF bands for testing whether multiple samples (chains or runs) share a distribution ([Säilynoja et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-022-10090-6)); tree-ESS and visualizations comparing independent runs with Monte Carlo error taken into account ([Magee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07629)); rank-normalized R-hat ([Vehtari et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08008)).
- Non-reversible parallel tempering, as an alternative or complement for multimodal targets, also gives normalizing-constant estimates. — [arXiv 1905.02939](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02939)

### Inferences
- When independent SMC runs land in different modes, that is direct evidence that the SMC Monte Carlo error is much larger than within-run diagnostics suggest. Within-run ESS cannot detect a mode that no particle reached. The practical protocol: K ≥ 4–8 independent runs. Report the spread of (a) log-evidence estimates, (b) structural marginals (gate or edge inclusion), and (c) key burden parameters across runs. Test agreement with Säilynoja multi-sample bands or rank-normalized R-hat applied to *run-level* summaries. If runs disagree, either fix the moves (bigger structural moves, tempering) or report a stacked or mixed posterior that is labeled as not exact (Yao et al.).
- Discrete or structured SMC fails when proposals or rejuvenation kernels ignore correlations: Schäfer & Chopin's independent-component proposals, and grow/prune-only moves in trees (Section 3). For decision graphs, rejuvenation kernels should include moves that change a gate and its dependent steps together.
- Within SBC, SMC output should be resampled to equal weights (or weighted ranks used) before computing ranks. SBC is then also a test of whether the SMC run-level error is acceptable at the chosen particle count.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that specifically defines a "mode-coverage" diagnostic for SMC over discrete structures beyond cross-run comparison. The waste-free and Lee–Whiteley single-run variance estimators do not address modes that no particle reached; this is my inference, not their claim.
- The rule "use K independent SMC runs and compare log-evidence" is standard practice, but I did not retrieve a specific primary source stating it as a recommendation.
- Hoff, Raftery & Handcock (2002), "Latent Space Approaches to Social Network Analysis," *JASA* 97(460):1090–1098, is verified as a citation ([Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214502388618906)). Its content is **not** verified: the PDF available to me was a scan with no extractable text. The often-cited points are that latent positions are identified only up to rotation, reflection and translation, and that posterior samples are aligned by Procrustes transformation. These are relevant because SBC test quantities for such models, and for label-switching posteriors (Stephens 2000, "Dealing With Label Switching in Mixture Models," *JRSS-B* 62(4):795–809, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9868.00265), verified as a citation only), must be invariant functions such as distances or edge probabilities. They should be checked against the paper before use.
