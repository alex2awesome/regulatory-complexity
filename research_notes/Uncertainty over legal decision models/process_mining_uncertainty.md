## Q1. Stochastic process mining: stochastic Petri nets, stochastic discovery and conformance (Leemans, Burke/Leemans/Wynn, van der Aalst; the survey)

### Takeaway
"Stochastic process mining" (mainly Leemans, Polyvyanyy, van der Aalst, Burke and Wynn) adds probabilities to a control-flow model. These are routing weights on transitions (stochastic labelled or generalized stochastic Petri nets) and sometimes duration distributions. The model is then scored against the log's trace-frequency distribution, for example with the Earth Mover's distance or entropy measures. Almost all methods take a control-flow structure as given or discover it separately, then fit point estimates of the weights. The 2026 IEEE TKDE survey is the field's own map of uncertainty in process mining.

### Inferences
- In this literature, "uncertainty about the model" mostly means *aleatoric* routing and duration probabilities that are fit as point estimates, often on a fixed control-flow skeleton. Weight estimation, optimal discovery, Wasserstein tuning and causal-net weights all take that skeleton from a conventional miner. This differs from the project's *epistemic* posterior over which steps, orderings and loops a jurisdiction uses.
- The conformance measures (EMSC, entropic relevance, stochastic precision/recall) compare trace-language distributions. They could serve as posterior-predictive checks for a Bayesian process model, but they assume full traces, not milestone-only records.
- Mannhardt et al. 2023 (data-dependent weights) is the closest existing object to the project's "process model jointly with the decision model" goal. In it, routing probabilities are functions of case attributes.

### Gaps
- Apart from the TKDE 2026 paper, I found no verified stand-alone article titled "stochastic process mining survey". I read only the TKDE abstract, so the four levels and their classification of each method are not in these notes.
- I could not read abstracts for Burke et al. 2021 (reduction/abstraction), Rogge-Solti & Weske 2015, or the entropy-based conformance papers. They appear as title-only entries.
- Van der Aalst's textbook treatment of stochastic Petri nets was not checked.

## Q2. Uncertain, incomplete, partially ordered or censored event data (only some milestones observed)

### Takeaway
Process mining has a well-developed line of work on uncertain event logs, led by Pegoraro and van der Aalst. It represents imprecise timestamps, activity sets and case-membership as non-deterministic or probabilistic attributes, then computes bounds on conformance or probabilities of trace realizations. Separate lines resolve partial orders into probability distributions over total orders (van der Aa et al.) and impute missing events and timestamps using a stochastic process model plus a Bayesian network (Rogge-Solti et al.). Right-censoring appears only in predictive monitoring (survival analysis on ongoing cases), not in discovery.

### Inferences
- The project's data, a few administrative milestones per permit plus forum narratives, is a case of *massively missing events*, not of imprecise attributes. In process-mining terms the nearest relatives are Rogge-Solti et al. 2013 (impute missing events and timestamps from a timed stochastic model via a Bayesian network) and Moreira 2015 (BN inference when only a subset of tasks is observable). Both assume the process model is known or learned from complete data. Neither infers the structure from milestone-only data.
- Pegoraro's framework records uncertainty as *sets or intervals* attached to events and returns *bounds*, plus probabilities of realizations in the 2021/2022 follow-up. A forum report like "it took 3–5 months after resubmittal" could be encoded as an uncertain event under this taxonomy. The output would still be bounds or realization probabilities, not a posterior over the process model.
- Van der Aa et al. 2020's distribution over total orders is a reusable likelihood component when milestone dates tie or are coarse (e.g., same-day stamps).
- Right-censoring of open permits is treated only in predictive monitoring (Baskharon et al.). I found no discovery method that uses censored case durations to inform structure or step durations. This looks like a real gap that the project fills.

### Gaps
- I found no process-mining paper on interval-censored milestone dates, or on combining administrative records with self-reports or narratives as separate data sources with different bias and selection.
- I did not read the full texts of Rogge-Solti 2013 to confirm how the Bayesian network handles duration priors or which distributions it uses.
- Pegoraro's PhD thesis (RWTH) was not located or verified.

## Q3. Bayesian or probabilistic process discovery: Bayesian networks, HMM/automata, MCMC, posteriors over model structure

### Takeaway
Bayesian machinery in process mining is used for (i) Bayesian networks over activity occurrence and timing for querying and diagnosis (Moreira 2015; Sutrisnowati et al. 2015; Rauch et al. ICPM 2024), (ii) simulation-based Bayesian inference of *weights* on a fixed stochastic model (Cry et al. 2025, ABC-SMC), and (iii) Bayesian *optimization* over candidate structures (Brockhoff et al. 2026). In what I searched, I found no published method that puts a posterior distribution over Petri-net or process-tree *structure*, for example by MCMC. Structure search remains likelihood-guided or metric-guided optimization, going back to Herbst (2000).

The paper uses "the probabilistic mechanisms of Bayesian Networks to model processes from the structural and temporal information given in event log data," modelling "cases in their entirety". It can "infer execution probabilities of activities at different stages and ... query probabilities of certain process outcomes." [CR][PUB] — [doi:10.1109/ICPM63005.2024.10680678](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICPM63005.2024.10680678)

The method detects loops from a dependency graph, decomposes it into BNs, adds event attributes as states, and checks structure with chi-square and local-Markov tests to estimate lateness probability. ### Inferences
- The closest published object to "posterior over a latent process model" is Cry et al.'s ABC-SMC posterior over **weights** of a fixed stochastic workflow net or process tree. Structure enters only through the miner that produced the skeleton. A posterior over structure (steps, orderings, correction loops) that is jointly informed by timing data appears unclaimed in the process-mining literature I could verify. The project should still phrase this as "we found no prior work," not as a proven first.
- Two process-mining building blocks are directly reusable for a structure posterior. The likelihood would be the model's stochastic language, approximated by simulation as in Cry et al. or computed exactly as in Leemans et al. 2024. The candidate structures would come from enumerating place or operator choices, as in Brockhoff et al. 2026 and Herbst 2000. Bayesian structure-learning MCMC from the BN literature (order or partial-order MCMC) is the natural import.
- The process-aware BN approaches (Moreira; Rauch et al.) give per-case posterior queries ("probability activity X occurred given observed Y"). This is the inference the project needs for unobserved steps, but they condition on a learned network, not a structural posterior.

- One possibly relevant record (Hasselt document server, [handle 1942/49547](https://documentserver.uhasselt.be/handle/1942/49547)) returned HTTP 503, so its content is unknown.
- I found no work giving Bayesian model averaging over process models, or credible intervals on structure-level quantities such as the probability that a loop exists.

## Q4. Queue- and duration-aware process models, waiting times, and inferring unobserved durations

### Takeaway
Queue mining (Senderovich, Weidlich, Gal, Mandelbaum) treats queues as first-class entities and discovers queueing networks from event logs, including scheduled processes. The business-process simulation line (Rozinat et al. 2009; Camargo, Dumas, González-Rojas 2020; Fracca et al. 2022) fits duration and resource models and can impute missing start times by matching simulations to the log. Waiting-time decomposition (Lashkevich et al. 2023) splits observed transition delays into causes. None of these is Bayesian, and all assume a mostly complete log.

- Senderovich, Weidlich, Gal, Mandelbaum. "Queue Mining – Predicting Delays in Service Processes." CAiSE 2014, LNCS (title only) [CR] — [doi:10.1007/978-3-319-07881-6_4](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-07881-6_4)
- Senderovich, Weidlich, Gal, Mandelbaum. "Queue mining for delay prediction in multi-class service processes." *Information Systems* 53 (2015) 278–295. The paper establishes "a queueing perspective in process mining" for online delay prediction. Its predictors "treat queues as first-class citizens" and are either regression-based or "directly grounded in queueing theory." ### Inferences
- The queue-mining view matches the project's rule of keeping elapsed waiting separate from service time. Senderovich's work models delay as queueing, i.e. a resource or backlog effect, and not as an intrinsic step duration. - Lashkevich et al.'s cause taxonomy for waiting time, Chapela-Campa & Dumas's extraneous delays, and batch detection all give named mechanisms for why a step's clock is long. These could become explicit latent components, not one lumped duration.

## Q5. Decision mining and DMN discovery, and uncertainty in mined decision logic

### Takeaway
Decision mining grew from decision-point classification on Petri nets (Rozinat & van der Aalst 2006; de Leoni & van der Aalst 2013, using alignments) to DMN discovery (Bazhenova & Weske 2016–2019; De Smedt et al. 2019). Uncertainty enters in three ways: overlapping, non-mutually-exclusive rules when decisions are non-deterministic or under-recorded (Mannhardt et al. 2016); fuzzy DMN for imprecision (Bazhenova et al. 2017); and data-dependent stochastic routing weights (Mannhardt et al. 2023). I found no Bayesian DMN discovery with posterior uncertainty over rules.

### Cited Findings
- Rozinat & van der Aalst. "Decision Mining in ProM." BPM 2006, LNCS pp. 420–425 (title only) [CR] — [doi:10.1007/11841760_33](https://doi.org/10.1007/11841760_33)
- de Leoni & van der Aalst. "Data-aware process mining: discovering decisions in processes using alignments." ACM SAC 2013 pp. 1454–1461. Earlier techniques "identify decision points, but do not analyze data flow". Here alignments of log, data and model create "a well-defined classification problem per decision point" so that "data flow and guards can be discovered and added to the process model." [CR][PUB] — [doi:10.1145/2480362.2480633](https://doi.org/10.1145/2480362.2480633)
- Mannhardt, de Leoni, Reijers, van der Aalst. "Decision Mining Revisited – Discovering Overlapping Rules." CAiSE 2016, LNCS pp. 377–392. Existing methods "assume that decision making is fully deterministic, and all factors influencing decisions are recorded." When rules overlap "due to non-determinism or incomplete information," the new decision-tree-based method discovers overlapping rules that "fit the recorded data better at the expense of precision." ### Inferences
- Mannhardt et al. 2016 states the identification problem plainly: "all factors influencing decisions are recorded" is the standard but false assumption. That supports modelling the project's statute decision points as probabilistic and not as deterministic guards when applicant attributes are unobserved.
- Fuzzy DMN covers *vagueness* (degree of membership), not *epistemic* uncertainty about which rule a jurisdiction applies. Data-aware stochastic Petri nets (Mannhardt et al. 2023) are the natural frequentist baseline to compare a Bayesian joint process-plus-decision model against.
- Decision mining assumes the decision outcome, i.e. the branch taken, is observed per case. In milestone-only permitting data the branch (e.g., whether a correction loop happened) is often latent. The project's joint inference therefore needs latent-branch EM or MCMC, as in Ferreira & Gillblad's EM for latent case IDs, and not classification.

### Gaps
- I found no Bayesian or posterior-uncertainty DMN discovery method, and no work on uncertainty quantification (confidence or credible intervals) for mined guards. A targeted search on "decision mining" with "uncertainty" on arXiv returned nothing.
- Rozinat & van der Aalst 2006 and the 2018/2019 BPMN-to-DMN papers have no abstracts in these notes.
- Bazhenova's PhD dissertation ("Discovery of Decision Models Complementary to Process Models," Potsdam 2018) appeared in search results ([HPI page](https://hpi.de/en/research/publications/dissertations/dissertation-ekaterina-bazhenova.html); [PDF](https://publishup.uni-potsdam.de/files/41002/diss_bazhenova.pdf)), but I did not open it. It is verified at snippet level only.

## Q6. Applications to government, permitting and administrative processes

### Takeaway
The most directly relevant public benchmark is the **BPI Challenge 2015** log of building-permit applications from five Dutch municipalities over about four years. ### Inferences
- BPIC 2015 is a candidate external validation set for the project. It is real municipal building-permit event data across five jurisdictions, with dense activity logs.
