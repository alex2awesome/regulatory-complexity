AB 2533, as a Process

The same statute seen from the applicant's side of the counter: what the law requires to happen, what four jurisdictions say happens, what one city's own documents show it designed, and what permit records can observe. Companion to the decision-model dashboard, Pricing “Adequate.”

1 · What the statute mandates

Gov. Code § 66311.7 mandates process elements and no procedure. Highlights mark the spans the skeleton parse anchored each element to (required, optional / “may”). Below it, the skeleton as swimlanes.

2 · What four jurisdictions declare

Each city's published program page, transcribed step by step. Solid boxes are steps that instantiate a statute-mandated element; dashed amber boxes are the city's own inventions. Lanes are actors; a change of lane is a handoff. These are declared processes: what the city tells applicants, not necessarily what it does.

3 · Coverage of the mandated elements

Which of the statute's elements each declared process mentions. A gap can mean the city doesn't do it, does it but didn't say so, or the element is implicit (“decision”). Gaps on the applicant-protective elements — the third-party option and the notice of it — are the ones closest to real omissions, because an owner cannot discover those through the generic permit process.

4 · One city's designed process, from its own documents

A records pull for Hayward found no council staff report, resolution, ordinance, or fee-schedule line creating the program (2,108 council matters 2023–2026 and four adopted fee schedules checked). The program is administrative: it existed before the statute (January 2024, $590, the “Certificate of Compliance” already in place), was adapted from Berkeley's program (its form still cites Berkeley Municipal Code § 19.40.040), and was retrofitted to AB 2533 during 2025. Handouts, a questionnaire, and archived page versions yield the richer process below.

5 · What permit records can observe

Scan of open permitting data (2026-09-04). Only San Francisco exposes per-review-station timestamps; only Los Angeles and San José label AB 2533 permits explicitly. The confidential third-party inspection is unobservable everywhere, by design.

How to read this page

Three tiers of evidence, none derivable from the statute alone. Declared = what a city publishes for applicants (authoritative for applicant burden and terminal artifacts; silent on internal routing). Designed = what the city's own documents say the departments will do (staff reports, resolutions, handouts; for Hayward, handouts only). Observed = what records show happened. Element tagging on this page is keyword-based and will be replaced by a parse of each city's steps through the same prompt as the skeleton. All artifacts and sources: corpus/ab2533/process/.
