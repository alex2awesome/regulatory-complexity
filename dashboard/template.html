The idea in ten seconds: a toy law, parsed

A two-line ordinance: “A resident may keep a backyard chicken coop if the coop stands at least ten feet from any neighboring dwelling, is maintained in sanitary condition, and complies with the State Animal Welfare Code.” Parsed, it becomes:

Inputs

distance_to_dwelling — resident measures it (site sketch)
coop condition facts — clerk observes on one visit
→

Conditions

#1 OBJ distance ≥ 10 ft — computed from the measurement
#2 JUDG “sanitary condition” — the clerk's call
#3 REF “complies with the Animal Welfare Code” — another law, not expanded here
→

Outcomes

approve  deny

Read-out: size = 3 conditions; judgment share = 2 of 3; burden ≈ one self-measurement + one clerk visit; discretion = every coop that passes the tape measure lands on “is it sanitary?” — the answer depends on which clerk shows up. To price that phrase, we generate coops sweeping from spotless to squalid, ask many committed clerk-personas to rule on each, and report the interval where they disagree. The rest of this page does exactly this, for a real law.

What this is

Goal. Measure what a rule costs the people subject to it, and eventually simplify rules while changing as few outcomes as possible. We split “complexity” into three separately measured costs: size (how much there is to read), discretion (how often the text fails to determine a case's outcome), and burden (the time, money, and waiting it takes to produce what the process demands). Existing complexity metrics capture only the first; the middle one is the measurement this project adds.

Method, briefly. We have language models compile the statute into executable decision trees — several times, independently, under strict contracts: every condition must quote its statute span verbatim, one condition per subdivision, inputs must be raw observable facts, and every input names the real-world act that produces it. We never trust a model's opinion about the law; models only generate artifacts (trees, test cases, readings), and every artifact is checked by execution. Running a case through a tree explores both branches of every judgment condition, so the result is the set of outcomes the text licenses — a case with more than one licensed outcome is a concrete witness of discretion. To price a single vague word, we generate synthetic units sweeping a severity axis and ask a panel of committed reader personas to apply the predicate to each unit; each reader's cutoff is estimated from their decisions, and the spread of cutoffs is the word's looseness. Burden is priced per acquisition event — one inspection yields many facts — from effort-class priors until fee schedules and permit records replace them.

Validation without ground truth. The instrument is calibrated on planted worlds where the right answer is known by construction (a threshold rule must compile to a threshold; an XOR rule must defeat every simple program — it does). Compiled trees face a reconstruction test: a fresh reader applies the raw statute to the same case and must land inside the tree's licensed set. The external test is a natural experiment: California required every city to publish a § 17920.3 checklist, so hundreds of independent human compilations of this same statute exist — our looseness measure should predict where those checklists disagree. That comparison is the next milestone.

Where this sits. Legal-complexity metrics (Katz & Bommarito's work on the U.S. Code; Colliard & Georg's regulation-as-code measures) count textual structure and cannot see discretion — “adequate” is one token. Rules-as-code, from the 1986 British Nationality Act in Prolog through Catala and OpenFisca to recent LLM formalization work (SARA's hand-written gold Prolog, the Dutch government's production permit decision models, JusticeBot's pathways), treats a formalization as a means to correct answers, and explicitly suppresses the fact that a statute admits several viable formalizations. We measure as signal exactly what that literature controls as a confound. Administrative burden research (Herd & Moynihan; sludge audits; the federal burden-hour inventory) supplies the burden axis; reform microsimulation (PolicyEngine, EUROMOD) supplies the who-gains-who-loses machinery for the simplification half.

Terms used on this page. Fuzz panel — test cases made by drawing every input at random from its allowed values; good for exercising a tree's wiring, deliberately unrealistic (a coin flip per defect makes every unit a disaster), so population-level claims wait for panels calibrated to real housing data. Identified set — all outcomes a case can validly reach through the text. Judgment mass — the share of conditions requiring an official's judgment; sensitive to how finely the tree is parsed, unlike the behavioral discretion measure. Band — the severity interval where committed readers disagree.

Problem register

Ranked. The instrument is honest about what its numbers do not yet mean.

Cross-reading divergence reads 0.000 — an artifact, not a finding.

The three readings name their input facts almost entirely differently (only construction_date is shared by all three), so one test panel is not a coherent world across trees. Stricter prompting made this worse, not better — finer-grained facts multiply the naming surface — so a field crosswalk to shared latent facts is unavoidable, plus adversarial case generation. The vocabulary divergence is itself a finding: faithful readers don't even agree on what facts the statute asks for.

Ensemble validity: seven personas, one model, scripted by us.

The band could partly be persona-design artifact. Fix: real readings — scrape 10–20 city § 17920.3 checklists as checklist-sourced readers, later revealed readings from permit records. Also: batched elicitation protocol and sweep-grid quantization; no per-case replication yet.

Placeholder priors under every population and burden number.

The 9.5% band mass uses a beta(2,5) guess; burden hours/dollars use class priors. Calibration paths: assessor / inspection data; PRA-ICR burden inventory, fee schedules, permit timestamps.
