The same toy law, as a process model

The ordinance says what makes a coop legal and nothing about how a resident gets one: no form, no inspector, no order of steps, no document at the end. The town of Millbrook fills that silence. Solid boxes are what the law fixes; dashed amber boxes are what the town invented. Lanes are actors; a step in a new lane is a handoff.

Read-out: 6 activities, 3 actors, 4 handoffs, 1 rework loop, terminal artifact "coop permit." The law fixed three conditions; the town invented the form, the sketch, who checks what, a single animal-control visit, a checkbox standing in for an entire other law (imposed thinness, in miniature), the loop, and the permit itself. The next town over could satisfy the same ordinance with a registration postcard and no inspection at all: one decision model, two processes. That gap is process discretion, and it is why the process layer is measured from what towns publish and do, never parsed from the statute alone.
