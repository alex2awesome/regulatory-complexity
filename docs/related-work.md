# Related work, by axis

Compiled 2026-09-02 from session searches and paper reads. Annotations state what each item measures and what it misses relative to our three-axis decomposition.

## Size — structural/textual complexity (the measured axis in prior work)

- **Katz & Bommarito**, *Measuring the Complexity of the Law: The United States Code*, Artificial Intelligence & Law (2014). Structure, entropy, interdependence of the U.S. Code. Text-only.
- **Ruhl & Katz**, *Measuring, Monitoring, and Managing Legal Complexity*, 101 Iowa L. Rev. 191 (2015). [SSRN 2566535](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2566535). The agenda-setting piece: complexity claims should be empirically testable.
- **Colliard & Georg**, *Measuring Regulatory Complexity* ([AEA 2024](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2024/program/powerpoint/QiSbDRZ4)). Treats regulation as source code — operator/operand counts, cyclomatic-style measures on Basel rules. Closest economic prior; static, cannot see discretion ("adequate" is one token).
- **Coupette, Hartung et al.**, *Law Smells: Defining and Detecting Problematic Patterns in Legal Drafting* ([arXiv:2110.11984](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.11984)).
- **Katz, Coupette et al.**, *Measuring Law Over Time* ([arXiv:2101.11284](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.11284)). Network-analytic framework, U.S. and Germany.

## Discretion — vagueness, standards, and formalization multiplicity (our contribution)

**Theory:** Hart's open texture (*The Concept of Law*); Endicott, *Vagueness in Law*; **Kaplow**, *Rules versus Standards: An Economic Analysis*, 42 Duke L.J. 557 (1992) — ex ante vs. ex post specification cost, our normative frame; Daston, *Rules* — thin vs. thick rules.

**Linguistics:** **Kennedy** (2007), relative vs. absolute gradable adjectives — the typology behind the static screener; **Sassoon**, multidimensional adjectives. NLP precedent: **Lebanoff & Liu**, *Automatic Detection of Vague Words and Sentences in Privacy Policies*, EMNLP 2018 ([D18-1387](https://aclanthology.org/D18-1387/)) — ratings-based word-level vagueness, not consequence-based. Caution from legal commentary: [ambiguity is not established by disagreement alone](https://www.abajournal.com/columns/article/ambiguity-in-law-its-not-established-by-disagreement-alone) — hence our adjudication step.

**Statute formalization (all treat multiplicity as a confound; we measure it):**
- **SARA**: Holzenberger, Blair-Stanek & Van Durme (2020), [repo](https://github.com/SgfdDttt/sara); *Factoring Statutory Reasoning* ([arXiv:2105.07903](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.07903)). Nine IRC sections + gold hand-written Prolog — our known-answer test and planted-thin control (sections deliberately edited to be unambiguous).
- **Jurayj, Holzenberger & Van Durme**, *LMs and Logic Programs for Trustworthy Financial Reasoning* ([arXiv:2508.21051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21051)). QA/abstention on SARA; gold statute Prolog fixed; names multiplicity ("several may be viable") and sets it aside; disagreement priced as referral cost.
- **Sadowski & Chudziak (SOLAR)**, *On Verifiable Legal Reasoning* ([arXiv:2509.00710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00710), [prompts](https://github.com/albsadowski/solar)). Statute → single frozen ontology + Python; "single ontology… isolating structured reasoning effects from ontology quality variations." Prompt techniques we adopted (numerics/logic firewall); anti-pattern we avoid (fitting the formalization to training answers).
- *From Legal Text to Executable Decision Models* ([arXiv:2604.17153](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17153v1)) — 95 Dutch production permit decision models as gold; structural similarity ≠ outcome equivalence.
- *Span-Grounded Deontic Trees / NormBench* ([arXiv:2606.08932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08932)) — 2,290 provisions; recursion decay, auditability trap.
- IRC inconsistency detection via formalization ([arXiv:2511.11954](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.11954)).
- **Janatian et al.**, *From Text to Structure* ([arXiv:2311.04911](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.04911)) + **JusticeBot** ([arXiv:2308.02032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.02032)) — human-built legal pathways as gold.
- **LegalBench** ([arXiv:2308.11462](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.11462)) — rule-application tasks incl. SARA; tests application, not formalization.
- Metamorphic testing of tax software: Tizpaz-Niari et al. ([arXiv:2205.04998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.04998)); LLM-agentic follow-up ([arXiv:2509.13471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13471)) — the oracle-free validation machinery we import, pointed at statutes.
- Lineage: Sergot et al., *The British Nationality Act as a Logic Program* (CACM 1986); **Catala** (Merigoux et al., ICFP 2021); **OpenFisca** ([github](https://github.com/openfisca)) / PolicyEngine.
- **Spangher et al.**, *LegalDiscourse* (NAACL 2024, [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.472/)) — our segmentation layer: TEST/EXCEPTION/DEFINITION spans, ~100K state laws, trained taggers.

## Decision-logic complexity — validated metrics to port (saved 2026-09-02)

The case in brief: logic-complexity measurement exists and is *validated* in adjacent fields; it never reached law because law lacked a machine-readable decision model — the parse is the missing precondition. Stage 3 ports, it does not invent.

**Software.** McCabe cyclomatic complexity (1976; independent paths through control flow), Halstead measures, modern "cognitive complexity." Decades of validation against defect rates.

**Business-process models — the strongest precedent and a validation source.** [Mendling, *Metrics for Process Models* (Springer 2008)](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-540-89224-3): analyzed thousands of real process models (incl. the ~600-model SAP reference set), found formal-error rates of ~10–20%, and fit models predicting **error probability from complexity metrics** — size, density, connector heterogeneity, structuredness, connector mismatch. Led to the Seven Process Modeling Guidelines (Mendling/Reijers/van der Aalst): fewer than ~50 elements, minimize routing paths per element, structured over unstructured, avoid OR-splits — i.e., empirically derived drafting guidelines for decision logic. Also [Cardoso's control-flow complexity](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-89224-3_4) (XOR/OR/AND splits weighted by branching burden) and a [systematic review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/311863131_Complexity_Metrics_for_Process_Models_-_A_Systematic_Literature_Review). **Validation idea (Mendling→Joyce):** these metrics predict *human error* in process models; Joyce's Denver/Seattle rework data gives actual error/rework rates per provision — test whether ported metrics predict permit rework. That re-validates the metric→error link on legal material, a result in itself.

**Decision models proper.** [DMN complexity measurement](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-11641-5_41) (NOD/NOE/TNDO counts, cyclomatic complexity on decision-requirement diagrams; validated against perceived complexity). Behind it, the older decision-table literature (Vanthienen et al.): completeness/exclusivity/consistency verification of decision tables — directly applicable to score sheets and checklists. DMN also supplies the vocabulary for our Stage-0 schema split (decisions vs. condition atoms vs. input data).

**Law.** Colliard & Georg ≈ Halstead/McCabe transplanted onto statute *text* — right instinct, wrong object: text conflates drafting style with logical content (NY vs. SD encode similar constructs in wildly different surface forms; Ho's Figure 13 is the proof). Katz & Bommarito measure text structure, not decisions.

**Welfare side (existence proof for tree surgery).** [Bhargava & Manoli, AER 2015](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.20121493): IRS field experiment (35,050 filers); simplifying the EITC notice — including *deleting eligibility screens satisfied by all recipients*, literally pruning informationally-inert conditions — raised take-up from 0.14 to 0.23. Complexity reduction has measurable welfare effects; frictions like stigma-reduction did not.

## Rules ↔ decision models: the mapping, and decision-model complexity (saved 2026-09-02)

**The rules→decision-model mapping has ~65 years of history, in four traditions** — all treating it as one-valued (one law → one model); none measures the *family* of models a text permits (our angle):

1. **Legal logic & drafting**: Layman Allen (from 1957) — symbolic logic for drafting/interpreting statutes, "normalized drafting" to make the logical structure explicit. The intellectual ancestor. *(knowledge-cited; spot-check before paper use)*
2. **Decision tables for administration** (1960s–70s OR literature): statutes and procedures hand-converted to decision tables; verification (completeness/consistency/redundancy — Vanthienen's lineage) and *optimization* — Reinwald & Soland (1966) on converting tables to optimal test-ordering programs (the ancestor of our burden-minimizing condition reordering); Hyafil & Rivest (1976): constructing optimal decision trees is NP-complete — rule simplification is intractable in general, justifying heuristic/LLM-proposed edits. *(knowledge-cited)*
3. **Rules-as-code practice**: Sergot's BNA (1986) → OpenFisca/Catala/Blawx; and actual government methodology — the Dutch wetsanalyse practice that produced the 95 production permit decision models used as gold in arXiv:2604.17153; NZ Better Rules.
4. **NLP/LLM extraction**: pre-LLM, the KU Leuven line — [Text2Dec (Etikala, Van Veldhoven & Vanthienen)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-66498-5_27), [decision models from process descriptions](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-85469-0_8), [deep-learning DMN extraction](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362956508_Extracting_Decision_Model_and_Notation_models_from_text_using_deep_learning_techniques), [survey of acquisition methods](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-82153-1_17) — plus the 2025–26 LLM statute-formalization papers listed above.

**Decision-model complexity analysis exists in four bodies:**

1. **DMN metrics** (thin; perceived-complexity validation only — above).
2. **Decision-table theory**: verification + minimization + optimal condition ordering (above).
3. **TCS decision-tree complexity**: for a Boolean function f, D(f) = minimum depth of any decision tree computing it; related measures (certificate complexity, sensitivity, degree). Payoff for us: a statute's decision *function* has an **intrinsic complexity** (minimum questions any faithful process must ask), and the parse's actual size minus that lower bound = **drafting overhead** — separating necessary complexity from representational excess. Caveat (Dan): some overhead is welfare-functional (salience/safe harbor), so overhead ≠ waste. *(knowledge-cited)*
4. **ML tree pruning**: CART's cost-complexity pruning — minimize R(T) + α·|T|, error plus α per leaf — is the exact formal template for Stage 6's welfare-constrained simplification, with our R being asymmetric, population-weighted harm. *(knowledge-cited)*

## Official decision models of law — additional validation corpora (saved 2026-09-03)

Agencies themselves publish decision models of the statutes they administer. Each is one *authored member of the family of readings* — usable both to validate our parses (compare tree vs. official tree) and to measure discretion (multiple official operationalizations of the same law = the city-checklist move at scale):

- **[GOV.UK smart-answers](https://github.com/alphagov/smart-answers)** — open-source (MIT) Ruby repo of the UK government's live question-flow decision trees for statutory questions (benefits, employment rights, holiday entitlement calculations). Machine-readable government-authored trees + the underlying legislation = statute↔tree pairs at scale. *(verified)*
- **IRS Interactive Tax Assistant + form worksheets** — the IRS's own online decision trees ("Do I qualify for the EITC?") and the 1040 worksheets (hand-authored decision tables/calculations of the IRC). Pairs directly with our SARA work: same code, agency-authored model. *(knowledge-cited)*
- **German Programmablaufplan Lohnsteuer** — the Finance Ministry publishes the *binding official algorithm* (flowchart/pseudocode) for wage-tax calculation annually; statute + official algorithm + yearly versions = law-change tracking on an authored decision model. *(knowledge-cited)*
- **FDA Food Code + standard inspection form** — the model code both NYC's and San Diego's schemes derive from; the shared ancestor gives the health-code crosswalk a natural construct ontology and a third "reading" to triangulate NY/SD against. *(knowledge-cited)*
- **Benefit screeners**: Benefits.gov Benefit Finder, SSA BEST, HealthCare.gov screener — federal eligibility decision trees; **docassemble**/court-form interview corpora (open-source guided interviews = decision trees for legal processes); Australia's DataLex (AustLII) and CSIRO Data61's Regulation-as-a-Platform encodings. *(knowledge-cited)*

Two-sided use: (a) *parser validation* — our tree vs. the agency's authored tree for the same provision; (b) *discretion measurement* — where several authored models exist for the same higher-level law (states implementing a federal program, cities implementing CalCode, annual versions), their divergence is observed family-spread, no LLM involved.

## Rules-as-code corpora — deep dive (validation assets)

- **OpenFisca** ([github](https://github.com/openfisca)): open-source tax-benefit rules engine begun by the French state (~2011); Python; law modeled as *variables* (formulas over person/household entities) + time-versioned *parameters* (rates/thresholds in YAML) with legislation references; country packages (France is the largest; New Zealand used it in Better Rules; others). Powers citizen-facing eligibility simulators (Mes Aides lineage). Uses for us: (1) compute ported complexity metrics over whole country packages → the first empirical distribution of decision-logic complexity across a welfare state, granularity-controlled because it's code; (2) coder interpretation choices (issues, comments) = revealed compilation decisions.
- **PolicyEngine** ([github](https://github.com/policyengine), [policyengine-us](https://github.com/policyengine/policyengine-us)): nonprofit fork of OpenFisca-Core; **US model: 95,000+ parameters, 5,500+ variables**, income tax for all 50 states + DC plus major federal/state benefits; active microsimulation over enhanced CPS with distributional reform analysis (who gains/loses — the harm half of our simplification loop, already built). **The validation gift: per-variable YAML test suites** — thousands of (household facts → expected amount/eligibility) cases. Parse the statutes/regs behind a program (EITC, SNAP), run their tests through our trees: SARA-style ground truth, but at scale and on *unsanitized* law.
- **Catala** ([Merigoux, Chataing & Protzenko, ICFP 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.03198)): a DSL designed for law around **default logic** (Lawsky's insight: statutes = general rules + overriding exceptions), literate programming pairing statute text with code, lawyer–programmer pair "compilation"; French **family benefits fully formalized** (compiled to an OCaml library + web simulator), housing-benefits work with the French administration, US IRC §121 as demo. Uses for us: (1) default-logic exception semantics is the right formal account for our EXCEPTION/score structures; (2) their documented lawyer-programmer disagreements = qualitative non-codability data; (3) small expert-validated statute↔code gold pairs. Two adjacent gems from the same ecosystem: [static detection of *date-arithmetic ambiguities* in law](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.08935) (formalization surfacing genuine statutory ambiguity — a machine-found discretion witness) and [CUTECat: concolic execution for computational law](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18212) (automated test-case generation over coded law = principled adversarial case panels, directly relevant to our divergence testing).

## Agency structure, delegation & handoffs (saved 2026-09-04)

**Structured data on formal agency design:** [Selin & Lewis, *Sourcebook of United States Executive Agencies* (ACUS)](https://www.acus.gov/projects/sourcebook-united-states-executive-agencies-second-edition) — 55 statutory characteristics for ~106 federal agencies, **with downloadable Excel data + codebook**; the US Government Manual (agency functions/subunits, govinfo XML). **Agency manuals as public process-layer text**: IRS Internal Revenue Manual, SSA POMS, USCIS Policy Manual — the agencies' own statute→procedure→role elaborations, at massive scale.

**Delegation measured from statute text — the learnability precedent:** Epstein & O'Halloran, *Delegating Powers* (1999) hand-coded how much discretion Congress delegates per statute; [Huber & Shipan, *Deliberate Discretion?* (2002)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/deliberate-discretion/F956D78FBE9D97375F3560458862F34E) — statute detail as inverse discretion, cross-state (Medicaid) and cross-national; modern NLP versions: [Vannoni, Ash & Morelli, *Measuring Discretion and Delegation in Legislative Texts* (Political Analysis)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/abs/measuring-discretion-and-delegation-in-legislative-texts-methods-and-application-to-us-states/281BC160DE55EE6A214C570F713EFC6A) and [Anastasopoulos & Bertelli, *Understanding Delegation Through Machine Learning* (APSR, EU application)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/abs/understanding-delegation-through-machine-learning-a-method-and-application-to-the-european-union/1724F3ECFA1F0AABE3C7F8DA5C5D521B). So statute-text → delegation *amount* is an established, now-ML task. **Unclaimed: predicting the operational handoff graph** (which desks, what sequence) and measuring statute→manual→practice drift.

**Observed handoff data:** municipal open-permitting datasets with per-review-station timestamps (SF DBI, NYC DOB, etc. — each application row shows which desk touched it when = the handoff graph in the wild); Joyce's Denver/Seattle rounds-of-review collaborations; agency case-processing statistics.

## Burden — the time tax

- **Herd & Moynihan**, *Administrative Burden: Policymaking by the Other Means* (2018) — learning/compliance/psychological costs, our cost taxonomy's ancestor.
- **Sunstein**, *Sludge* / sludge audits — the audit framing for §2 of our intake.
- Paperwork Reduction Act burden-hour inventory: [CRS IF12673](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12673) (overview); **[GAO-18-381](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-18-381)** (errors found: one ICR ~$270M under, another 12M hours over — why we use it ordinally/differentially); GAO-05-424, GAO-06-974T; [ACUS PRA report](https://www.acus.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Draft-PRA-Report-2-15-12.pdf) (2012).
- **IRS Taxpayer Burden Model** ([Pub. 5743](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5743.pdf)) — the one real predictive burden model: survey-calibrated microsimulation over return characteristics. Text→hours prediction remains essentially unclaimed.
- Reform microsimulation (harm side of simplification): **PolicyEngine**, EUROMOD, TAXSIM — who gains/loses from a *given* reform; no search over simplifications.
