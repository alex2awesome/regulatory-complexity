## Stage 0 — Schema v3: decisions vs. conditions (the representation fix)

Dan's critique: "condition" is under-defined. Split the representation, borrowing DMN (Decision Model & Notation) vocabulary:

- **Condition atom**: a span-pinned testable proposition (unchanged from today, still typed OBJ/JUDG/REF).
- **Decision node**: a choice point with an owner (decider), an outcome effect, and an explicit **aggregation logic** over its condition atoms. Node types:
  - `branch` — boolean over atoms (today's tree node);
  - `score` — weighted sum of atom indicators vs. threshold(s) (REQUIRED for NY/SD point systems and grade cutoffs; a binary tree cannot represent them);
  - `table` — DMN-style decision table (rows of atom combinations → outcomes).
- **Input operationalizations** (Dan's "age can be defined 5 ways"): each input field gains a list of defensible operationalizations + who fixes the fact; input-level looseness = count/divergence of these, measured like predicate looseness.
- Crosswalk objects map at the **decision/construct level**, not the atom level — the NY/SD granularity difference is the same decision atomized differently.

Deliverable: `decision_tree.schema.json` v3 + executor support for `score` nodes + migration of the AB 2533 compile prompt.

## Stage 1 — Validation infrastructure on already-coded law (tax / SARA)

The only place with real ground truth. Runs:
1. Parse SARA's 9 sections with our pipeline (schema v3).
2. **Behavioral validation with real answers**: run SARA's 376 cases through our parsed trees; compare to gold answers (and to gold Prolog execution). This replaces the LLM-referee reconstruction check with actual ground truth — faithfulness with teeth.
3. **Codable-fraction statistic v1** (Manning's question): per provision, OBJ/JUDG/REF classification + best-code fidelity + program size; report % codable / % articulable-only / % discretionary, with costs.
4. **Negative control**: looseness instrument on SARA predicates must read ≈0 band (sections were edited to be unambiguous); nonzero = instrument noise floor.
5. **Scale-up option — PolicyEngine YAML tests as ground truth**: policyengine-us (95K+ parameters, 5.5K+ variables, per-variable YAML test cases of household-facts → expected amounts) gives SARA-style validation on *unsanitized* law at scale — parse the statute/regs behind one program (EITC or SNAP), run their test suite through our tree.

## Stage 3 — Decision-logic complexity metrics (traditional measures, applied)

Nobody has applied validated logic-complexity metrics to law because law lacked machine-readable decision logic; our parse supplies it. Port from adjacent fields:
- **McCabe cyclomatic complexity** on the parsed decision graph; size/depth/fan-in.
- **Process-model metrics with empirical validation**: Mendling's error-prediction suite (density, connector heterogeneity — the one literature that links model complexity to human error rates), Cardoso's control-flow complexity.
- **DMN complexity metrics** (NOD/NOE/TNDO, CC on decision requirement diagrams).
- Report at stated parse granularity (judgment mass lesson: structural metrics are granularity-sensitive).
- Bonus corpus: OpenFisca/PolicyEngine coded rules — compute the same metrics over already-coded eligibility law for cross-domain norms (first empirical distribution of decision-logic complexity across a welfare state).
- **Mendling→Joyce validation**: Mendling's metrics predict human error in process models; test whether they predict permit rework rates (Joyce's rounds data) — re-validates the metric→error link on legal material.
- Adversarial case generation precedent: CUTECat (concolic execution over Catala programs) — principled test-case generation for coded law.

## Stage 4 — Burden v2 (Dan: burden is under-defined)

Decompose burden into three parts, each with its own estimator:
1. **Direct effort** (current instrument): facts × acquisition acts, priced per act, calibrated vs. fee schedules / PRA burden-hour inventory / IRS TBM.
2. **Rework & delay**: extend the tree to a process graph with resubmission loops; expected delay = P(provision triggers a rework round) × round-duration distribution, estimated from administrative data (Joyce's Denver/Seattle rounds-of-review data; public permit timestamps). The avoidable-comment metric (% of round-k comments answerable from round-k−1 information) attributes rework to provisions. We never predict one project's delay; we estimate the expectation and quantiles.
3. **Carrying cost of elapsed time**: for construction, delay converts to dollars via financing costs (construction-loan rates × months) — coarse but defensible.
Also keep: wasted burden (effort by ultimately-denied cases), sensitive disclosures (separate count).

## Stage 6 — Welfare-constrained simplification demo

On the richest artifact: propose edits (reorder, prune, thin a standard, or substitute an LLM-adjudicated step) and price each as Δcomplexity vs. Δoutcomes on case panels — with two constraints from the meeting: (a) LLM substitution is scored as *re-delegation* (its executor variance is measured discretion, not free simplicity); (b) information-redundant conditions are pruned only if also welfare-inert (no salience/safe-harbor role) — proposed empirical proxy (Alex's idea): dispute/appeal rates.
