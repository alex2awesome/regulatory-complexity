# ADU permitting: the seam is in the guidance layer, not the law

2026-09-22. First substantive findings from the ADU workstream. Every number
here is backed by a quote that replays byte-exact from a retained capture;
verification counts are stated per layer. Collection is still running, so counts
are current-as-of, not final.

## The result

California's ADU statute mandates ministerial approval within 60 days. We
measured three layers per jurisdiction — **ordinance → published guidance →
applicant experience** — and the burden is not where a de jure reading would put
it.

| | ordinance (41 codes) | guidance (30 cities) |
|---|---:|---:|
| pre-application requirement | **1 (2%)** | **14 (47%)** † |
| discretionary review or hearing | **0 (0%)** | — |
| affirmatively ministerial / by-right | 22 (54%) | — |
| correction-cycle language | — | 12 (40%) † |
| stated action deadline | 22 (54%) | 11 (37%) † |

**The codes are clean.** Zero of 41 captured ordinances impose discretionary
review, a hearing, or an unambiguous ADU-level owner-occupancy requirement under
a strict same-sentence test. Exactly one (Vallejo) codifies a pre-application
requirement, and it is a deed restriction before occupancy.

**The guidance is not.** Fourteen of thirty cities publish an applicant-facing
requirement to obtain planning approval *before* a building permit application
will be accepted:

> "all JADU and ADU requests require approval of a completed ADU/JADU
> application **prior to submitting plans for Building Plan Check**"
> — City of Orange

> "For ADUs over 800 square feet, you must obtain Planning Division approval
> **before submitting a building permit application**."
> — City of Vallejo

† **Correction, 2026-09-22.** The guidance counts were first reported as 17 / 13 / 14.
A sibling agent's cleanup script deleted this packet's retained captures from a
shared directory (it built its keep-list from its own state files only, so it
classed every other agent's renderings as orphans). Recovery re-fetched all 108
pages and hash-checked each one: 68 came back byte-identical, 10 had changed
since capture and are held separately rather than rebound to old quotes, and 24
returned 403. The figures above count **only claims whose supporting quote still
replays against a byte-identical recovered source**: 683 of 972 quotes. Three
further pre-application claims (Thousand Oaks, Ventura, Antioch) are recorded but
currently unverifiable, and Antioch's document is the state HCD handbook in any
case. Both lead quotes (Orange, Vallejo) are source-verified. The ordinance
column is unaffected.

That gate is not in the code. It is administrative practice occupying the space
between statute and applicant, and it is invisible to every dataset that
measures permitting.

## Refinement from the top-30 cities (added 2026-09-22)

The largest 30 cities were captured separately (419 quotes, all independently
re-verified, junk-screened). They qualify the pre-submittal-gate finding in a
way that matters.

**Los Angeles does not document planning-before-building as its default path.**
For an ordinary ADU, LADBS treats planning as a clearance *raised during its own
plan check* (IB P/BC 2026-150): "Clearances will be generated by plan check
during the plan check process and must be approved by relevant departments
before a permit and Certificate of Occupancy are issued." Planning-first is
documented only for the Coastal Zone ("a discretionary filing and public hearing"
— ZA Memo 143 Q.23) and Specific Plan areas such as Mulholland. So the LA
applicant quoted above ("you can only submit an application for building and
safety once you have approved plans from planning department") describes the
coastal/specific-plan path, not the citywide rule. That is a more precise claim,
not a weaker one: the gate is **conditional on geography** in LA, and a clock
starting at deemed-complete hides it exactly where it applies.

Across the top 30, a pre-application requirement is **required** in 5 cities
(Santa Clarita: "Clearance from the Planning Division is required before any
plan submittals can be reviewed by Building & Safety"; Oxnard; Bakersfield; San
Francisco's local program; Sacramento's handout), **conditional** in 6
(coastal-zone-only in Long Beach and Huntington Beach), and **advisory** in 7.
Sacramento's own documents disagree with each other — one handout requires
planning approval first, its ADU Resource Center says none is needed — which is
the guidance layer contradicting itself.

The three-way split (required / conditional / advisory) should replace the
single yes/no count in the table above when the two city batches are merged;
the ranks-31-60 count has not yet been re-coded on that scale.

## Why it is invisible, in the data's own words

HCD's Annual Progress Report is the only per-project ADU timing census in
California — 358k Table A rows, 922k Table A2, ~74k joinable ADU projects across
~399 jurisdictions. Its own data dictionary defines the start of the clock:

> `APP_SUBMIT_DT` — "Date the application was submitted to the jurisdiction.
> **If the application was incomplete at the time of submittal, this will
> reflect the date the application was determined to be complete by the local
> government.**"

So the measured interval begins at deemed-complete. Everything the guidance
layer adds upstream of that — planning approval, surveys, utility
pre-conditions, counter visits — is structurally outside the measurement. An
applicant states the mechanism as a rule:

> "You can only submit an application for building and safety once you have
> approved plans from planning department."

And another states the consequence for their own clock:

> "I hired them 12 months ago but we went back and forth with the city before it
> was officially submitted as an ADU project."

Three independent layers agree: the dictionary defines the truncation, cities
publish the gate that fills it, applicants describe living through it.

## What that does to a compliance estimate

Against the 60-day statutory deadline, using the APR clock and excluding
degenerate rows: **45% of jurisdiction-years exceed it** (365 of 811, median of
medians 52 days, p25/p75 16/106).

That is a **lower bound**, and two defects push the true figure higher:

1. **The clock starts late** (above). Pre-submittal time is not counted.
2. **Degenerate reporting.** 328 jurisdiction-years across 121 jurisdictions
   report a single date for both milestones, so their whole duration
   distribution collapses to zero. Oakland reports a **median of 0 days across
   1,005 ADU projects** while an Oakland applicant reports 18 months. The
   largest ADU jurisdiction in the state contributes unusable data that anchors
   the compliant end of any statewide estimate.

A reconciliation pass on the clock file: five identities hold (project-type
labels partition joined rows; durations are a subset of joined rows; quantiles
ordered; closed 6-value unit vocabulary), one fails (the collapsed
distributions), and one residual is a measurement rather than an error —
**25.7% of joined ADU projects carry no recorded disposition**.

## The forum corpus: what it can and cannot carry

822 threads captured, 16,590 speaker-turns, 5,690 distinct speakers.
**2,690 quotes, 2,690 independently verified, 0 failed** across nine coded
slices, each with its own mutation-tested verifier plus one written from this
contract.

Can carry:

- **Pre-submittal burden** — 224 rows across 121 episodes. The region no
  administrative source can see. Examples: 261 days under contract with no
  application ever filed, the owner believing permits were pulled; PG&E
  requiring a full application, drawings and up to $2,500 of engineering before
  it will quote a price; ~2 years of planning destroyed by a one-street special
  zone discovered only after two counter visits.
- **Correction cycles, as bounded counts** — "back and forth 10 times with the
  HCD", then "3 months and 4 contact attempts" of silence after compliance; a
  round existing solely because a comment required the site plan redrawn at a
  different scale. (An earlier version quoted "each round adds 4 to 8 weeks";
  withdrawn 2026-09-23 — that post is byte-identical to one published 2.1 hours
  earlier under a different username, and is excluded as fabricated.)
- **Deterrence and abandonment** — projects abandoned on cost after research, at
  a utility problem, on a pro-forma; a Los Angeles variance never applied for
  because the contractor "knows what the city will say". These never enter any
  permit record, so the census is **selected on surviving the invisible stage**.
- **Undocumented gates** — address assignment holding a meter set, corroborated
  by a GC's claim and three Oakland owners, one where the post office stopped
  mail to both units because the building department never sent notification.

Cannot carry:

- **`completeness_review`.** Zero episode rows in 483 episodes. Established by
  exhaustive enumeration of r/AccessoryDwellings (957 posts, 6,195 comments,
  leaf windows tiling 2019-2026 with provably zero gaps), so this is a property
  of the source, not of retrieval. Applicants do not narrate intake, almost
  certainly because it is invisible to them — the same reason the census records
  it badly. One episode shows a second mechanism: a Pasadena applicant cannot
  report his own round count because his contractor held the file, so the count
  is unknowable to the person a survey would ask.
- **Magnitude without bounds.** Of 420 timing-and-count rows, only 76 carry an
  exact value; 115 are interval-censored and 229 are qualitative. Usable as
  censored data, not as point estimates.

## Threats we measured rather than assumed

- **Targeted astroturf.** Fabricated first-person *permit-timeline* posts —
  precisely our most-wanted evidence class, because "how long did your permit
  take" is the question that sells ADU services. One byte-identical pair under
  two usernames 24.3 hours apart (confirmed mechanically: 1 exact-duplicate
  group in 540 substantial submissions, 5 near-duplicate pairs above 0.55
  Jaccard, four of them benign same-author reposts). An extraction agent
  **retracted its own episode** on confirming it. A full rescan of 1,338 substantial submissions found exactly two identical-text
  groups under different usernames, both permit-timeline posts; one was the source
  of a correction-cycle quote this note originally cited, since withdrawn.
- **Non-independent sources.** An ordinance cluster of five LA-area cities
  (Downey, El Monte, Fontana, Lancaster, Norwalk) shares up to 874 distinct
  10-word sequences and a 452-character verbatim passage. Four cities' guidance
  reuses state HCD Handbook prose verbatim, and one city's "handbook" *is* the
  state handbook. 20 of 30 cities run the same CMS.
- **Layer contamination.** A de jure gate rejected five documents a crawler had
  already captured as ordinance text — LA's Zoning Code Manual and Commentary,
  SF Planning's ADU FAQ, the state HCD handbook hosted by a city, a county
  draft, a county handout. Filing any of them as code would have collapsed the
  two columns of the table above into one.
- **Keyword counts are not findings.** Three times, a keyword count produced a
  headline that careful extraction destroyed: discretionary review 22 → 0,
  owner-occupancy 26 → 0, and (in the CalFresh predecessor) a published-steps
  rate that was an artifact of what extractors chose to record.

## Retrieval, honestly

- Ordinances: 41 of 68 captured, **27 blocked**, every blocked record carrying a
  programmatically-checked disclaimer that it is not evidence no ordinance
  exists. American Legal and Code Publishing are Cloudflare-gated. Municode
  required reverse-engineering a five-call JSON API; two bugs there would have
  silently missed ADUs entirely (zoning lives in a *second* Municode product).
  **Los Angeles, San Francisco and Sacramento codes are unreached** — the top
  follow-up, since LA is the largest ADU jurisdiction and has the richest forum
  evidence.
- Guidance: 30 of 30 cities captured, but **14 sit behind Akamai** and 43 pages
  across 18 cities came from Wayback snapshots; for 11 cities the archive text is
  the only readable text. One JavaScript shell (HTTP 200, 19 characters) was
  classed `captured_but_no_text` rather than counted as publishing nothing.
- Forum: full-text search is quota-blocked at every limit. Title search works
  and is ~1,000x cheaper (88 posts for a 7.7-year range in one call versus 4,880
  date-scan windows for 128), at the cost of missing posts whose ADU mention is
  body-only.

## Where this sits against the literature

Three novelty reviews found the *representation* taken — a 2011 DOE/LBNL
questionnaire recorded ~19 permitting process elements as presence/absence for
>290 jurisdictions; WRLURI codes nine approval entities across ~2,450
communities; Terner already published ADU durations from this exact APR join;
Seattle published a within-stage decomposition for one city. What no source
does, and every source deletes by construction, is the **pre-submittal and
correction region**, and no existing dataset can address **selection into the
permit record** at all.

That is the claim this evidence supports: not a better timeline, but that the
measured population is conditioned on surviving a stage nobody measures, with
direct evidence of who does not survive it.
