`pipeline/procbench/solar_panel/fit_county_timing.py` implements the administrative
timing component. For each phase independently:

    log(reported cell median) = phase intercept
        + year effect + system-size effect + storage effect
        + geographic county effect [AHJs only]
        + actual authority effect + residual

Municipal and unincorporated-county authorities remain distinct. Utilities have
their own authority effects without county effects. County is a grouping of
authorities by geography, not a claim that a county government issues city permits.

The intercept has Normal(log(10), 1.5²) prior; size/storage effects Normal(0,.5²),
year effects Normal(0,.7²). County and authority effects have centered Normal
priors with learned scales. Their log scales have Normal(log(.5),.7²) priors;
the residual log scale has Normal(log(.7),.7²). These are explicit working priors,
not externally calibrated capacity distributions. Reference: 2024, 0–10 kW,
PV without storage. The model does not infer a causal installation-volume effect.

Known same-day zeros are left-censored below one business day. Among 58 zero
pre-installation-interconnection cells, 45 explicitly have no approval-to-build
requirement, seven are unresolved and six are measured zero durations with a
requirement. Only the last six enter the timing likelihood; the others stay in
typed structural/unresolved routes. Installs are **not** substituted for sample
size. Residual variation combines unexplained cell variation and unknown error
in a reported median.

Sampling uses exact conditional Gaussian draws, analytically integrating the
authority effects for the county/fixed-effect update, then redrawing those
effects before updating scales. Log-scale slice updates target an explicitly
log-scale prior. This is Monte Carlo posterior sampling, not an LLM posterior.
An exact small dense-Gaussian comparison agreed to approximately 10^-15 in
conditional means and 10^-17 in covariance. This was one focused mechanics
check, not a new verification campaign.

### Frozen holdout scoring (not a new fit)

`timing_training001/heldout_scores_v2.json` evaluates 3,418 held-out administrative
cells. Both models score the same coarsened outcome: values ≤1 business day are
a censoring event; larger observations have a density on log reported median.
This avoids comparing incompatible zero/positive observation supports. The
simple baseline is fitted to training data only and has a phase-specific zero
mass and positive-log Normal distribution. No LLM baseline is claimed here.

**The county model does not beat the simple baseline overall.** It improves
held-out-authority predictions but loses on the selected year holdout. This
evaluation does not justify a general claim that the hierarchy predicts better.

and common outcome support) are described in `HELDOUT_SCORE_V2_CORRECTION.md`.

**Chronology correction:** the panel's inherited `holdout_forward_year` flag
means 2022, from an earlier 2017–2022 analysis. The first county training run
also contains 2023–2024 data, so calling this a forward forecast was wrong.
No held-out cells entered training, but later years did: this is year
interpolation, not temporal extrapolation. The separate fixed-budget fit
`timing_training_through2021_001/` now implements actual chronological exclusion.

cells, mean log score is -1.17936 versus baseline -1.16909 (difference -0.01027);

(970 cells through 2021) improve +0.0783 in log score, with 89.9% coverage.
These preserve rather than replace the earlier results; no tuning followed.

descriptive, model-dependent variation in this administrative panel, **not

## Legacy result qualification

`county_model/SAMPLER_REVIEW.md` records two confirmed problems in the older
`solar_panel/fit_hierarchy.py`: scale-space priors sampled in log coordinates
without their Jacobian, and incompatible global-elasticity priors between two
transition kernels when random slopes are enabled. Existing historical output
was preserved. It should not be represented as posterior output under the
declared priors until corrected and rerun. The new component does not reuse
either kernel. The review's zero-admission issue in the new component was fixed
before the training/full runs; the review text is a record of the pre-fix code.
