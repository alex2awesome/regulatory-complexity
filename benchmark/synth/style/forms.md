# Style: application forms, checklists, questionnaires (distilled from El Cerrito AB 2533 checklist, Hayward pre-submittal questionnaire, Hayward handouts)

**Header block.** Agency name, division, address, phone; form title; "Effective <date>" or "Created: <m-d-yy>" and a page count ("Page 1 of 2"). Forms cite the statute they implement in the first sentence ("Effective January 1, 2025, in compliance with AB 2533, the following … Checklist is provided for homeowners seeking to legalize…").

**Sections.** Lettered/numbered: A. Project Information (APN, Address, Owner, Owner Address, Email, Phone, Date), B. Instructions (who fills it in — "The licensed contractor must ensure…"), C…N. Substantive items as numbered rows with Compliant / Violation / Description columns, then a **Signature block** (Owner / Licensed Contractor (license #) / City Inspector, with dates) and **"For Office Use Only"** (Received by, Date, Permit #, Fee paid) — the office box is where a form quietly reveals a back-office step (e.g., "Routed to: ☐ Planning ☐ Fire ☐ Code Enforcement"), so include such boxes ONLY when the spec's reveals license it.

**Questionnaires** (pre-submittal): 10–25 yes/no questions with "If yes, …" branches; they encode eligibility logic and sometimes leak the agency's decision rules ("If the unit was converted after 2020, stop — the amnesty program does not apply; see the standard ADU process").

**Handouts.** Bulleted requirements with "shall"; two options laid out ("There are two options available to the property owner… removal… legalize"); references to other handouts by name and URL; the tone is instructional and slightly dated (a "Created" date years before the program).

**Checklists** enumerate the inspected conditions (smoke alarms, CO alarms, egress, sanitation, electrical, structural…) as short imperative requirements with measurements ("minimum clear opening width of 20 inches"). 400–1,200 words.
