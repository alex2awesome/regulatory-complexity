# Style: municipal program pages, generic permit pages, FAQs (distilled from Hayward, El Cerrito, Lafayette, San Mateo Co. real pages, 2026-09)

**Shape.** Breadcrumb ("Home › Services › Permit Center › <Program>"), an H1 with the program's *local* name, an opening paragraph that cites the enabling state law by bill number and code section ("In response to AB 2533, Government Code Section 66332, the City of Hayward is offering…") and states the purpose in reassuring language ("to ensure these spaces are safe, healthy, and habitable"). Then headed sections in roughly this order: **Eligibility** (bulleted conditions with dates), **Process Overview** (prose or numbered steps; verbs "submit", "obtain", "schedule", "will issue"), **Required Submittals** (bulleted document list, "Other documents will be considered on a case-by-case basis"), **Fees** ("a flat fee of $570 in addition to applicable administrative and state fees. This fee is due prior to project review."), **Handouts / Forms** (links), **Contact** (department, street address, phone, email, counter hours). 250–600 words.

**Voice.** Second person ("you may obtain…"), passive for the agency ("applications will be reviewed", "staff will reach out with instructions"), modal precision matters: "must" = requirement, "may" = option, "will" = agency promise. Cross-references to *other* programs are common and are a natural place for contamination ("If you would like to receive a Certificate of Occupancy instead, see the ADU/JADU Permit page"). Pages often name the terminal artifact ("Certificate of Compliance", "Revocable Certificate") and describe what it does and does not guarantee.

**What pages typically reveal / omit.** Reveal: applicant steps, required documents, fees (sometimes stale), the department name, the terminal artifact, and *which* inspections happen. Omit: internal routing, reviewer counts, second sign-offs, referral to code enforcement, actual durations (timing, when present, is a target: "typically 2–4 weeks" — often optimistic). Generic permit pages describe the *machinery* (online portal name, "over-the-counter vs. plan review", "plan check comments", "resubmittal") that the program page relies on without repeating — this is why the one-hop rule matters.

**Staleness cues.** "Updated <date>" footers, an old fee, a department name that the fee schedule contradicts, a bill number without the later renumbered section.

**FAQ pages.** Q/A pairs, 8–15, in the applicant's words ("How long does it take?" "Do I need a contractor?" "What if my unit fails the inspection?"); answers hedge timing ("most applications are reviewed within 3–4 weeks of a complete submittal") and often disclose a rework loop ("if corrections are required, you will receive a correction notice and may resubmit").
